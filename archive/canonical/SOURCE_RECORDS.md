## Why the full canonical files are not automatically mirrored

A reproducible archive is not the same thing as indiscriminate publication.

Canonical working records can contain:

- private project context;
- unpublished hypotheses;
- personal material;
- heritage-sensitive coordinates;
- third-party images or text with unresolved rights;
- internal handoff material;
- superseded or incomplete records that require context.

The public repository therefore publishes the **method law, source fingerprints, public claims, provenance, evidence records and reproducible materials** needed to audit public research, while retaining a controlled boundary around material that has not passed a public-release audit.

## Release rule

A canonical source may be mirrored in full when:

1. its contents are public-safe;
2. rights are clear;
3. sensitive locations or personal information are handled appropriately;
4. publication status is explicit;
5. the release does not collapse working speculation into public evidence.
