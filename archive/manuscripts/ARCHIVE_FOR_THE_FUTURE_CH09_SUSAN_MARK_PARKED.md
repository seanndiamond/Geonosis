## Story setup

The lion, reeds, hand and home/return glyphs appear on Susan's holographic projection. Mark challenges why the lion is labelled an L when the depicted scene suggests a trait-vector relation. Susan invites him to ask ORI for original phonetic derivations.

## Scene sequence

1. **The lion has a sound.** Mark reports its conventional L value. Susan asks how it was derived. ORI gives the Ptolemy–Cleopatra comparison.
2. **Not the original marks.** The displayed signs are standardized renderings. Mark demands original artifact photographs and region-to-sign matches.
3. **The Greek context precedes the Egyptian value.** ORI explains the independently readable Greek names, inferred cartouche identifications, and Greek/pedestal vs hieroglyphic/shaft distinction on the Philae Obelisk.
4. **The missing bridges.** Mark challenges one vs two reeds, variant T signs, sign order and omitted/additional marks; ORI substitutes general accounts and assigned-value tables for a complete derivation.
5. **Alexander and Berenice.** Historical comparisons extend guessed name matches. Mark insists on independent checks, not another table of expected letters.
6. **Correction finally stated.** ORI acknowledges it has not completed the artifact-to-sign-to-sound audit. Susan distinguishes this narrow failed demonstration from a universal proof that phonetics cannot occur.
7. **The Book of the Dead returns.** Susan brings up independent papyri and the group's proposed interpretations: half-circle/home, loop/conscious return, bird/trait, hand/operator, eye/sensory acuity, lion/trait vector, feather/reptilian trait. Mark notices the visual operations vanish in normalized transliteration though they remain visible in the source.
8. **Rekhmire operational scene.** Operator, implement, action and proposed glyph correspondence are examined in context as a scene-to-glyph derivation, not merely shape resemblance.
9. **The register.** They preserve original images, transformations, competing readings, variations, unresolved issues and tests. Neither accepted phonetics nor the pictographic interpretation escapes scrutiny.
