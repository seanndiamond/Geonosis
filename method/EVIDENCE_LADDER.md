# Evidence Ladder and Proof Hurdles

Geonosis separates the strength of a claim from the confidence of the person making it. A claim advances only when it clears the hurdle for the next room.

## Level 0 — Source existence

**Claim:** the source exists and has been correctly identified.

Required:
- stable source reference or provenance record;
- correct site, artefact, document, image, body, date or observation identity;
- disclosure of crop, rotation, enhancement, stitching, annotation or other transformation;
- source-integrity check where AI-generated or altered material is possible.

Fails if the key source is misidentified, unavailable, fabricated, or materially altered without disclosure.

## Level 1 — Direct observation

**Claim:** a visible feature, relation, absence, measurement or sequence is present.

Required:
- exact location in the source;
- plain description before meaning;
- justified scale/orientation/viewing conditions;
- distinction between source feature and annotation.

Allowed language: `shows`, `contains`, `repeats`, `touches`, `faces`, `changes`, `is absent`.

## Level 2 — Candidate operator

**Claim:** a visible form behaves as an operator such as enter, divide, bind, guard, release, emerge, transfer or transform.

Required:
- constrained context;
- adjacency or relational behaviour;
- recurrence within the corpus or strong local sequence;
- plausible alternative operators stated;
- no dependence on an isolated attractive resemblance.

## Level 3 — Local grammar

**Claim:** multiple operators form a structured visual or spatial grammar.

Required:
- more than one operator class;
- sequence, boundary, slot, direction or state transition;
- a model of what the grammar permits and forbids;
- negative or control examples;
- explicit reject conditions.

Fails if missing steps must be invented to make the story work.

## Level 4 — Cross-case recurrence

**Claim:** the same operator or grammar recurs across multiple examples in a corpus.

Required:
- independent cases;
- comparable coding rules;
- distribution not explained by one copied source or one repeated annotation convention;
- recurrence at the smallest defensible claim scope;
- record of failures and non-matches.

## Level 5 — Cross-corpus convergence

**Claim:** partly independent corpora return compatible operator or procedural grammar.

Required:
- each corpus must first be read locally;
- comparison must not be created by renaming different forms after the fact;
- chronology, geography and transmission possibilities must be considered;
- resemblance alone is insufficient;
- serious alternative models must be compared under the same evidentiary rule.

## Level 6 — Predictive hypothesis

**Claim:** the model predicts something not used to construct it.

Required:
- prediction logged before inspection of the target evidence;
- expected feature, context or relation specified;
- pass/fail result recorded;
- failed predictions retained in the archive.

This is a major hurdle. Prediction converts a reading from retrospective fit toward testable research.

## Level 7 — Independent reproducibility

**Claim:** another observer can recover the central observation or operator mapping.

Required:
- source package available;
- instructions sufficient but not leading;
- independent observer record preserved;
- agreement and disagreement both logged;
- ideally blinded or semi-blinded tests for high-risk pattern claims.

Fails if only the originating observer can recover the proposed structure.

## Level 8 — External technical validation

**Claim:** a material, chronological, linguistic, biological, geological, engineering or other specialist proposition survives independent domain testing.

Possible evidence:
- laboratory result;
- survey or measurement;
- microscopy;
- provenance confirmation;
- specialist epigraphic derivation;
- geophysical or geological testing;
- statistical comparison;
- peer review;
- independent replication.

External validation does not make every higher interpretation true. It validates the proposition actually tested.

## Level 9 — Historical or civilisational reconstruction

**Claim:** a coherent historical process, technology, belief system or civilisational narrative is reconstructed.

Required:
- lower evidentiary levels relevant to the claim have been cleared;
- chronology does not contradict the sequence;
- causal steps are distinguished from analogy;
- missing evidence remains visible;
- conventional and alternative explanations receive the same audit burden.

This level should never be smuggled backward into Level 1 language.

## Level 10 — Speculative or metaphysical extension

**Claim:** consciousness-field, spiritual, cosmological or other high-order interpretation.

Rules:
- label explicitly;
- do not use it as proof of Levels 0–9;
- identify which lower-level observations motivate it;
- preserve philosophical or experiential value separately from public empirical proof.

## Governing principle

**Strength belongs to the evidence room, not to institutional prestige, rhetorical confidence, or habitual hedging.**

A visible fact should not be weakened because its implication is controversial. A grand hypothesis should not be strengthened because it is exciting.
