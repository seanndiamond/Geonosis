# Literature Context and Novelty Boundary — ODRR-RR-001

## Why this file exists

ODRR must not be presented as if science has never noticed that orientation changes visual perception. A substantial literature already addresses orientation-dependent object recognition, mental rotation, perceptual grouping, figure-ground organization, scene gist, familiarity and repetition.

The confirmatory question is therefore narrower:

> Do the **pre-identified pilot image families** show greater orientation-linked relational reorganization, as operationalized by the preregistered ODRR score, than matched controls under blinded randomized conditions?

The possible novelty lies in the **specific operationalization, stimulus comparison and cross-source replication question**, not in discovering that rotated visual inputs can be processed differently.

## Relevant prior literature

### Orientation-dependent recognition and mental rotation

Tarr, M. J., & Pinker, S. (1989). *Mental rotation and orientation-dependence in shape recognition*. Cognitive Psychology, 21(2), 233–282. DOI: `10.1016/0010-0285(89)90009-1`.

This classic work demonstrates that shape recognition can depend on orientation and that transformations toward stored/canonical views can contribute to recognition. It establishes a major alternative explanation for any ODRR effect: observers may be engaging ordinary orientation-dependent recognition processes.

Harris, I. M. (2024). *Interpreting the orientation of objects: A cross-disciplinary review*. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 31, 1503–1515. DOI: `10.3758/s13423-024-02458-8`.

This review emphasizes that object identity and object orientation can be processed differently, and that orientation processing can be slower, attention-dependent and vulnerable to binding errors. ODRR terminology must therefore remain descriptive until it is distinguished experimentally from established orientation/binding phenomena.

### Perceptual grouping and figure-ground organization

Wagemans, J., Elder, J. H., Kubovy, M., Palmer, S. E., Peterson, M. A., Singh, M., & von der Heydt, R. (2012). *A century of Gestalt psychology in visual perception: I. Perceptual grouping and figure-ground organization*. Psychological Bulletin, 138(6), 1172–1217. DOI: `10.1037/a0029333`.

The Gestalt literature establishes that grouping and figure-ground assignment depend on structured image cues, experience and context. ODRR therefore cannot infer unusual encoding merely from a change in grouping.

### Global scene organization

Oliva, A., & Torralba, A. (2006). *Building the gist of a scene: The role of global image features in recognition*. Progress in Brain Research, 155, 23–36. DOI: `10.1016/S0079-6123(06)55002-2`.

Rapid access to global scene structure is established in the scene-perception literature. The pilot description “global scene acquisition before local object identification” is consequently not treated as unique evidence by itself.

### Familiarity, inversion, part-whole structure and repetition

Flowers, C. S., & Peterson, M. A. (2026). *Does exposure within an experiment affect the influence of familiar parts versus wholes on figure assignment?* Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, 88(2), 43. DOI: `10.3758/s13414-025-03179-3`.

This study directly compares upright familiar configurations, inverted versions and part-rearranged versions, and reports effects of orientation, inter-part connectivity and repeated exposure on figure assignment. It is particularly relevant to the ODRR pilot because the pilot itself recorded orientation, relational grouping and priming/re-entry effects.

## Competing explanations the confirmatory study must respect

A positive target-control difference could still reflect one or more of:
- ordinary orientation-dependent object recognition;
- canonical-orientation or mental-rotation costs;
- familiar-configuration priors;
- figure-ground reassignment;
- global image statistics;
- edge density or contrast differences;
- semantic or iconic source recognition;
- repeated-exposure/priming effects;
- enhancement or rendering differences;
- unmatched visual complexity.

The matched-control design, recognition report, raw/enhanced separation, Giza-excluded sensitivity analysis, randomization and quality-control gates are intended to reduce these alternatives, not declare them impossible.

## Novelty boundary

ODRR-RR-001 does **not** claim:
- that orientation-dependent visual processing is new;
- that perceptual reorganization proves deliberate encoding;
- that a coherent rotated percept proves historical intent;
- that ancient/terrain targets possess a categorically different visual mechanism from ordinary images.

A confirmatory survival result would support only this statement:

> Under the preregistered task and stimulus set, the pre-identified target images produced a larger observer-rated combination of coherence, grouping change and non-mere-rotation than their matched controls.

Further claims would require separate experiments.

## Methodological implication

Because the ODRR composite is newly constructed rather than an established psychometric scale, its construct validity remains open. Outcome-neutral quality controls provide an initial measurement gate, but external psychophysical review is required before the protocol is treated as final.
