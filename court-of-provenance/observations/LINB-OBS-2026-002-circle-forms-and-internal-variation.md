## Three evidentiary layers (do not collapse)

### A. Source observations

Several supplied original-photograph *crops* show enclosed circular or near-circular marks containing visibly different incisions: vertical strokes, short horizontal strokes, diagonal segments, asymmetric divisions, and multi-stroke arrangements. Their relative positions, directions and neighbouring signs differ. These are distinguishable observable geometries within the supplied pixels.

No statement is made here that all such marks are necessarily instances of one or two formally classified signs. Full source context and sign-by-sign standard palaeographic mappings are pending.

### B. Conventional sign table

The **ka** and **qe** entries are standardised drawn glyphs paired with conventional syllabic values. A core syllabary displays categories, not a photographic catalogue of every scribal token. The fact that source marks vary visually raises an investigable token-to-type mapping question. The mere presence of visual variants does **not** by itself establish an error in the phonetic assignments or in conventional treatment of scribal variants.

### C. Claimant's proposed pictorial reading

Diamond identifies various enclosed forms as **faces with differing expressions and directions of attention**, including examples provisionally described in supplied crop filenames as angry, authoritative, concerned, determined, disappointed, negotiating, smiling, uncertain, and thinking. These labels are **interpretive annotations**, not facts independently demonstrated by the file names. Each must be tied to a source image, exact mark, neighbour field and reproducible morphological account.

The challenge: **which source features are suppressed by standardising a mark, and do the differences form a consistent, predictive local visual grammar?**

## Proposed test / evidence staircase

1. Register source tablet identifier, collection, image attribution/licence, resolution, original orientation, and full object photograph for every crop. Preserve original unmarked image.
2. Assign each cropped circular mark a unique instance ID and a crop rectangle within its parent image. Record neighbouring signs, row/line and orientation.
3. Make a **blind geometric annotation** of outer boundary, number of internal strokes, endpoints, intersections, angle, line direction, damage, and uncertainty, without expressive labels.
4. Independently map each token to published Linear B sign catalogues (or explicitly record UNMAPPED), distinguishing sign categories from scribal variants and possible non-signs.
5. Separately test the face/expression reading: ask blinded readers for facial features, expressions, gaze directions, relational partners and confidence. Compare inter-rater agreement, alternative orientations and negative controls.
6. Test internal and cross-tablet recurrence, local sequence position, adjacency, transformations, consistency with competing explanations, and predictive performance on held-out examples.
7. Reproduce the classification from unannotated parent images. Preserve disagreement and supersession history.

**Fail/reopen triggers:** failure to locate the image or mark on a verified source tablet; clearer photos overturning a claimed stroke; material misclassification of photographed marks; failure of blind agreement/recurrence; or an independently better derivation.
