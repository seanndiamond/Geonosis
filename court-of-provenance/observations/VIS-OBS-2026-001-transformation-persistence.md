# VIS-OBS-2026-001 — Transformation Persistence in Pictographic Systems

**Status:** Court-recorded cross-corpus observation  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Court posture:** current finding within the corpora examined by the Geonosis / Reading the Ancient Archive research program; subject to supersession only by new evidence, contradiction, failed prediction, provenance collapse, methodological fault, or superior reproducible derivation.

## Finding

Across the corpora examined in this research program, pictographic systems continue to remain semantically productive under multiple geometric transformations and repeatedly return coherent, internally consistent information within their own corpus.

The observed transformation family includes, where tested:

- rotation;
- mirroring;
- overlay;
- scale change;
- relational recombination;
- whole-object / local-field shifts.

The relevant evidentiary feature is not merely that new images can be perceived after transformation. The finding rests on repeated return of **coherent information that remains compatible with the local grammar already established for the corpus**, rather than collapsing into contradiction or undifferentiated noise.

## Current corpus record

Transformation-survival has been observed within the project across substantial work involving:

- cuneiform and clay-tablet fields;
- Egyptian hieroglyphic imagery;
- the Phaistos Disc, including degree-based rotation, mirroring and overlay;
- Linear B, newly opened on 2026-10-01, where original, 180-degree and 90-degree orientations returned further coherent image-fields;
- Stonehenge;
- Sacsayhuamán;
- terrain / Geonosis readings;
- Nazca remains queued for formal mirror / overlay testing.

Maya material has not yet been subjected to the full rotation protocol and should not be represented as having passed tests not yet performed.

## Interpretation

The Court records the working structural conclusion:

> **Pictographic systems examined in the ancient archive are multidimensional, multi-informational systems in which form, orientation, relation, overlap, scale, position and transformation can all carry information while local images retain semantic identity.**

The transformations are therefore treated as part of the information architecture, not merely as tolerable distortions of a primary orientation.

## Method consequence

Future archive work should preserve and test:

1. original orientation;
2. 90°;
3. 180°;
4. 270°;
5. horizontal mirror;
6. vertical mirror;
7. original / transformed overlays;
8. mirrored overlays;
9. local crop;
10. whole-object field.

For each state record:

- coherent return;
- compatibility with local grammar;
- contradiction;
- noise / non-return;
- repeated motif;
- cross-corpus correspondence only after local reading is stable.

## Court rule

> **Do not flatten geometric transformation into noise before testing whether the corpus remains semantically coherent through the transformation.**

This note does not declare that every possible transformation of every object is meaningful. It records a repeated property of the corpora actually examined and establishes a mandatory transformation test where applicable.
