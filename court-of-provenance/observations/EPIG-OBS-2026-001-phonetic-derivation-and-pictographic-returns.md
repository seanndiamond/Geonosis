## Lamb sign on Met 11.217.19

The Uruk IV witness shows a rounded enclosure with an inner mark. The Uruk III flock account shows a squarer enclosure with an angular mark. At Fara, the hand copy shows a box with horizontal strokes crossed by an upright. The Drehem sign is flatter and retains the crossed-stroke arrangement. The enclosure is traceable across these selected examples, with the closest visual correspondence between Fara and Drehem. The transition between the early Uruk inner mark and the Fara strokes is unresolved. [Uruk IV, CDLI P000830](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/830) · [Uruk III, PCSL source for P003513](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/pcsl/signlist/AddSources/index.html) · [Fara, CDLI P010032](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/10032/reader/5990) · [Drehem, Met 11.217.19](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/322463)

The flock context and later lexical evidence support the lamb classification. The Chicago Syllabary records *si-la* alongside the sign and gives Akkadian *pu-ḫa-du*. This attests a reading in the later scribal tradition. Pronunciation at earliest Uruk remains unresolved. [Chicago Syllabary A 2480, reverse i 73](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dcclt/P391514.116)
