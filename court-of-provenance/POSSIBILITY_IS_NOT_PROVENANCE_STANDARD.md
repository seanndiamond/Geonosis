## Operating rule
1. A merely possible alternative is not positive evidence and shall not receive weight solely for being conceivable or conventional.
2. Equal burden is not equal evidentiary weight. Assess each proposition at the rung actually achieved.
3. If two encoding systems are alleged to operate in the same field, the coexistence claim requires its own derivation: identify the marks, map their functions, show the applicable rules of each system and test the joint explanation across relevant occurrences. Distinguish competing explanations from demonstrated coexistence.
4. Preserve negative demonstration outcomes and their exact scope. Reopen on the constitutional triggers, not repetition of a familiar assertion.
5. A visual return establishes the observable relationship to the extent reproduced. Original intentional encoding and historical narrative require additional support; the same distinction applies to linguistic inferences. No requirement that sound be visually inherent in a mark: reproducible corpus-level derivation is admissible.
6. Neither failure to demonstrate phonetics in examined exhibits nor successful visual recognition alone proves universal absence of phonetics. The claimant's stronger thesis remains explicitly recorded as a thesis, not silently substituted for the scoped findings.
7. Do not demand that a pictographic investigator disprove an unsupported hypothetical phonetic layer before continuing a visual investigation; equally, do not require a phonetic investigator to disprove every conceivable image.
