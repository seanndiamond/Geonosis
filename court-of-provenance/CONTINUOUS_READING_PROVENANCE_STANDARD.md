# Continuous Reading Provenance Standard

**Court rule:** COP-I-11 — No Isolated Excerpt / Continuous Reading Provenance  
**Status:** CURRENT OPERATING STANDARD / REOPENABLE  
**Effective date:** 2026-10-26  
**Applies to:** Geonosis pictographic/terrain archive, reports, plates, cross-corpus atlases, books, website, and Court exhibits  
**Authority:** Additive extension of COP-I-01 (source), COP-I-03 (observation/interpretation), COP-I-07 (supersession), and COP-I-09 (no invented bridge).

## Governing rule

**An excerpt is not an isolated reading.** In Geonosis reports, a selected image, crop, glyph, motif, or scene is a *return from an originating continuous reading* whose local lexicon, contextual sequence, and interpretation precede or frame the selection.

Excerpts may be grouped by a shared motif (for example *wave*, *impact*, *heating refuge*, or *cold aftermath*) because reproducing the full parent story for every reference would be impractical. **The act of thematic extraction does not replace, erase, or retrospectively invent the originating story.**

The Court must not characterize such material as contextless motif-hunting merely because the public exhibit shows an excerpt. Conversely, the existence of an asserted parent story is not by itself independent physical or historical confirmation of the claimed event. The source-derived observation, author's reading, and external verification remain distinct.

## Required chain

`source/capture → parent continuous reading → local lexicon and narrative sequence → selected image/excerpt → thematic return/motif collection → revisitable full parent reading → subsequent expansions/corrections`

For each excerpt, preserve or explicitly mark as not-yet-linked:

1. **Source:** original artefact, terrain location or capture, image provenance, date, and unmodified source where available.
2. **Parent:** stable reading ID, name, corpus and archived version at the time of excerpting.
3. **Context:** local lexicon reference, sequence position, relevant preceding and following narrative, and what the excerpt was doing in that story.
4. **Extraction:** stable excerpt ID, precise image/crop/plate reference, operator transformations or annotations used, original author caption and motif tags.
5. **Chronology:** when the parent reading was developed and when the motif was recognized; distinguish documented sequence from later reconstruction.
6. **Claims:** observed morphology, Geonosis interpretation, and independent empirical/historical evidence kept in separate fields.
7. **Return route:** working link or resolvable archive reference from motif card → excerpt → full parent reading → source exhibits.
8. **Revision lineage:** additive corrections, expansions, new plates and superseded interpretations without silent replacement.

When a parent or source has not yet been digitized, the record must say `PARENT_LINK_PENDING` or `SOURCE_PENDING`; **never invent missing links or parent content**. Missing navigation alone does not mean the excerpt was interpreted without context.

## Adjudication and selection rules

- The burden is to inspect the **parent narrative and context**, not evaluate an excerpt as though the excerpt were the entirety of the claim.
- Source observations may be examined independently, but the author's Geonosis reading should be reported with its actual originating context.
- Motif-based browsing is a **navigation index**, not the method by which parent meanings were created.
- Thematic reuse does not automatically establish independent corroboration: record whether returns originated in separate objects, separate local readings, repeated screenshots, or derived reproductions.
- Parent readings remain open to revisit and extension at any time; each published excerpt continues to reference the specific parent version from which it came, while also linking to the latest version.
- A subsequent change to the interpretation is a versioned development, not an erasure of the earlier record; Court reopening standards remain in force.
- This standard governs **provenance presentation and fair review**, not a blanket finding that any proposed catastrophe, population, technology or event has been independently demonstrated.

## Website navigation contract

**Browse motifs** (e.g., “wave”) → see excerpt cards with source and parent ID → open one card → see its exact position within the **full continuous reading** → browse previous/next scenes and local lexicon → inspect source image and version history → follow cross-corpus returns.

The index must support one reading contributing many excerpts, one excerpt having multiple motif tags, and connections among related readings. Stable IDs must survive changes of title or website design.

## Example: catastrophe returns

A selected wave plate from a terrain or pictographic reading has a parent story. The catalogue must record **where the wave arrived in that story**, its immediately preceding activities/scenes, subsequent aftermath (such as destruction or cold, where those are part of the reading), and the exact source exhibit. The public “wave atlas” is a set of such pointers, **not** a collection of freestanding readings.

## Court outcome

**COP-I-11 is a provenance rule concerning the actual structure of the author's reports and the way excerpts must be represented.** It preserves the full staircase, makes the archive inspectable, and prevents a selected return from being stripped of the continuity that generated its meaning.

**Show me the parent reading. Preserve the chain.**
