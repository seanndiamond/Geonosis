# Contributing to the Geonosis Archive

This repository welcomes agreement, disagreement, replication, correction, and counter-evidence.

The most useful contribution is not "I believe this" or "this is nonsense." It is a **traceable result**.

## How to challenge a claim

For a specific Geonosis claim, provide:

1. the record, paper, figure, site, artefact, or source you are addressing;
2. the exact feature or step you dispute;
3. whether your disagreement concerns source identity, observation, operator, grammar, chronology, causation, or speculative extension;
4. your alternative explanation;
5. the evidence that favours it;
6. the Geonosis reject condition or proof hurdle you believe is triggered;
7. what result would make you revise your own alternative.

A criticism with its own failure condition is especially valuable.

## How to reproduce a visual reading

Whenever possible:

- begin from the raw source rather than an annotated image;
- record scale, orientation, crop, camera height and other viewing conditions;
- state what you saw before reading the proposed interpretation;
- identify operators or relations independently;
- then compare against the archived mapping;
- report agreement, partial agreement, disagreement or inconclusive result;
- retain your notes even if the result is negative.

For pattern-recognition tests, blind or semi-blind procedures are preferred when practical.

## How to submit a new case

Use `schema/evidence-record.schema.json` as the target structure.

Minimum viable case:

- source provenance;
- direct observation;
- claim level;
- alternatives;
- at least one failure condition;
- transformation disclosure;
- current status.

## What the archive will not do

- erase a failed test;
- turn disagreement into hostility;
- promote an attractive resemblance into a system without constraint;
- treat conventional authority as proof by itself;
- treat unconventionality as proof by itself;
- use AI output as an oracle;
- invent missing data to make a case complete.

## Corrections

If a contributor demonstrates an error, the preferred response is:

1. preserve the original record;
2. add a correction;
3. identify the evidence that forced the change;
4. update the current state;
5. thank the person who made the archive harder to fool.

## Tone

Attack the derivation, not the person.

The archive is intended to be adversarial toward claims and hospitable toward careful observers.
