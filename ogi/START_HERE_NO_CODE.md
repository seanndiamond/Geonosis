# Start Here: No Coding Required

You do **not** need to be a programmer, AI researcher, or GitHub expert to contribute to OGI.

If you have a real problem from work, family, community, research, training, business, education, trades, farming, health care, transport, sport, animals, government, or everyday life, you can bring that problem to the project.

The simplest contribution is often:

> **This keeps going wrong. Here is what people are being told to do. Here is what actually gets rewarded. Here is what happens instead.**

That is useful OGI evidence.

## The five-minute route

1. Create a free GitHub account if you do not already have one.
2. Open the Geonosis repository: `https://github.com/seanndiamond/Geonosis`
3. Click **Issues** near the top of the page.
4. Click **New issue**.
5. Choose **OGI: Real-world problem / question**.
6. Answer the plain-language questions.
7. Click **Submit new issue**.

That is enough. You do not need to download code, create a branch, run tests, or know what a pull request is.

## What kind of problem should I bring?

Anything where the visible behaviour may not tell the whole story.

Examples:

- A lawn-mowing crew is told to finish faster, and lawns start getting damaged.
- A nurse is measured on speed, while patients need time and attention.
- A school rewards test scores, and teachers start teaching the test instead of the subject.
- A delivery driver is rewarded for on-time arrivals and begins taking unsafe shortcuts.
- A dog is called disobedient, but the command, environment, reward, fear, restraint, or handler behaviour may be contributing.
- An AI system completes the task, but only by exploiting a loophole in how success was measured.

You do not need to know the answer.

A cleanly described problem is already a valuable contribution.

## What we will ask you

We keep it simple:

1. **What is happening?**
2. **Who or what is being blamed?**
3. **What were they told to do?**
4. **What actually gets rewarded or punished?**
5. **What information, tools, restrictions, pressures, or relationships surround the situation?**
6. **What would a better outcome look like?**
7. **What would change your mind about your explanation?**

If you do not know one of these, write **I don't know**. Unknown is a valid state.

## What happens after I submit it?

Your issue becomes a public research question.

People may:

- ask for more detail;
- compare the case with OGI's Field Audit;
- propose alternative explanations;
- test whether the problem is really caused by the actor, the surrounding field, or both;
- suggest a benchmark or experiment;
- build a technical test from your real-world example.

You do not lose ownership of your experience just because somebody technical later writes code around it.

## Want to go further?

If you are comfortable with GitHub or software development, read [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md). That explains forks, branches, replications, architecture challenges, tests, and pull requests.

But none of that is required to ask a useful question.

## One rule

Describe what you observed as clearly as you can.

Do not worry about sounding technical.

**Bring the problem. Show us the field. We can build the technical language together.**
