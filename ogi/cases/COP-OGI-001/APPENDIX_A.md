## A.1 Why this case exists

This appendix does not attempt to settle the global glyphosate debate.

It tests whether OGI can behave as the research partner it was designed to become when faced with a difficult real-world question containing:

- direct lay observation;
- uncertain local facts;
- toxicology;
- animal exposure;
- ecological effects;
- conflicting epidemiology;
- regulatory disagreement;
- active scientific reassessment;
- commercial interests;
- institutional funding relationships;
- political disclosures;
- alternative interventions;
- and strong prior beliefs.

The subject could have been toothpaste, diesel exhaust, school testing, food additives, workplace noise, or almost any other sufficiently complex system.

Glyphosate was chosen because an ordinary observation beside a river rapidly opened into all of these layers at once.

The benchmark is therefore not whether OGI reaches a conclusion favourable or unfavourable to glyphosate.

The benchmark is whether OGI can preserve the governing method while the case becomes complicated.

---

## A.2 Methodological status of the Court of Provenance

The Court of Provenance is **not a court of law**.

It has no statutory authority, judicial power, regulatory function, or legal standing. It cannot determine liability, compel evidence, issue enforceable findings, or replace courts, regulators, medical authorities, scientific institutions, or environmental agencies.

It is called a court because of its **method**:

1. state the proposition;
2. identify the evidence offered for it;
3. show the provenance;
4. separate observation from interpretation;
5. admit contrary evidence;
6. distinguish what has been searched from what has not;
7. challenge the derivation;
8. preserve unresolved states;
9. record corrections;
10. reopen when new evidence earns reopening.

The Court therefore acts here as an experimental evidence-adjudication subsystem within OGI.

## A.3 The originating observation

The inquiry began with a dog walker, not a toxicologist.

Sean Diamond regularly walks his dog, Donny, along an informal side of a Victorian river where people and dogs use tracks through the vegetation. At various times he observed workers carrying backpack sprayers and wearing substantial protective equipment while spraying vegetation beside the river and walking paths.

On one occasion he asked two workers what they were spraying. They described it as herbicide used for weed control.

Diamond's originating questions were essentially:

> Are these workers making the river better?
>
> Is the intervention looking after the people, dogs and wildlife that use this place?
>
> What is the actual problem being solved?

Later discussion associated this weed-management practice with glyphosate. However, this OGI run **did not retrieve the exact council work order, product, formulation, concentration, target weed species, spray rate, weather conditions, or river reach for the observed application**.

That distinction is governing.

### Direct observation

- workers sprayed vegetation beside a public riverbank/path;
- workers used substantial personal protective equipment;
- workers said they were applying herbicide for weed control;
- treated vegetation/path areas were later observed dead or browned.

### Interpretation or hypothesis requiring evidence

- the exact product was glyphosate;
- Donny absorbed it through his paw pads;
- the application harmed Donny;
- the application contaminated the river at harmful concentrations;
- the council's management program was unnecessary or non-compliant.

OGI preserves both the observation and the hypothesis without silently turning one into the other.

# A.6 Claim-by-claim adjudication

## Finding A1 — Glyphosate is biologically active by design

**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

Glyphosate is an herbicide whose intended function is to disrupt susceptible plant biology. Glyphosate-tolerant crops are engineered specifically so that the crop can survive labelled glyphosate applications.

This establishes biological activity.

It does **not** establish that every non-target exposure causes injury.

---

## Finding A2 — Some glyphosate-based formulations present greater non-target and aquatic hazards than glyphosate alone

**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

Australian regulatory history itself distinguishes the active ingredient from commercial formulations. APVMA's waterway review found that some formulations had undesirably high aquatic toxicity associated with surfactants and imposed restrictions/label changes.

Recent animal and aquatic reviews/meta-analyses also report non-target effects and formulation-dependent toxicity.

A contrary 2021 ecotoxicology review concluding generally low environmental risk under good agricultural practice was retained rather than omitted.

**Court boundary:**

`some formulations / studied exposures show adverse effects`

is not equivalent to

`every labelled use creates unacceptable environmental harm`.

---

## Finding A3 — Australian pet dogs are measurably exposed to glyphosate

**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

A 2026 Australian biomonitoring study detected glyphosate in the urine of 68% of 71 sampled pet dogs and AMPA in 13%.

The study therefore supports:

`DETECTED → EXPOSURE_SUPPORTED`

It does not by itself support:

`POISONING → CLINICAL HARM → CAUSATION`.

The study did not establish the dominant exposure route and identified no dog-specific acceptable daily intake.

### Donny-specific finding

**Status:** `UNRESOLVED`

No sample from Donny or the originating riverbank was tested in this inquiry.

Therefore the case does not establish that Donny was exposed at that site, how any exposure entered his body, or that the observed application harmed him.

---

## Finding A4 — Paw absorption remains a hypothesis, not a case finding

**Status:** `UNRESOLVED`

Dog paw pads are living, innervated and vascularised tissue, but are also heavily keratinised barrier structures.

The first OGI discussion initially moved too quickly from paws contacting treated ground to systemic absorption through paw pads.

The fresh v0.3 run corrected that claim boundary.

Possible exposure routes include contact followed by licking/grooming, dietary exposure, household/environmental exposure, direct dermal uptake, water, or combinations of routes.

The dominant route in Australian pet dogs remains research debt.

This correction is deliberately preserved because **OGI's correction behaviour is part of the benchmark**.

---

## Finding A5 — Human carcinogenicity cannot be represented by a single institutional sentence

**Status:** `MIXED / UNRESOLVED`

The corpus contains materially different findings.

### Evidence supporting concern

- IARC classifies glyphosate as Group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans, based on limited human evidence, sufficient experimental-animal evidence and strong mechanistic/genotoxicity evidence.
- A 2019 meta-analysis reported an elevated NHL risk among the highest-exposure groups.
- The 2025 Global Glyphosate Study reported tumour increases in a lifetime rat study, including at its lowest tested dose.

### Evidence weakening a simple cancer claim

- the large Agricultural Health Study did not find statistically significant associations with solid tumours or lymphoid malignancies overall, including NHL;
- a 2021 updated meta-analysis found no overall NHL association, while not ruling out an association with DLBCL;
- APVMA, EFSA/ECHA and US EPA have historically reached regulatory risk conclusions different from IARC's hazard classification.

### Current-state trigger

As of the case date, European agencies are formally reassessing the 2025 Global Glyphosate Study and the US EPA is conducting a new 2026 human-health review.

Therefore the Court does not collapse the current state to either:

`glyphosate is proven harmless`

or

`glyphosate is proven to cause cancer in all real-world exposures`.

The claim remains decomposed by hazard, exposure, study design, dose, formulation and population.

---

## Finding A6 — Community biomonitoring establishes exposure fields more readily than single-chemical causation

**Status:** `SUPPORTED WITH LIMITS`

Studies from Mexico, China, Argentina and Brazil show that people living in agricultural environments can have measurable pesticide/glyphosate exposure and, in some studies, associated biological markers or health outcomes.

However, several of these environments contain **multiple pesticides and correlated agricultural exposures**.

OGI therefore retained these studies as evidence of exposure fields and hypothesis-generating health associations without silently attributing every observed outcome to glyphosate alone.

This was a direct test of the Claim-Type Controller.

---

## Finding A7 — Regulatory approval is not the same proposition as optimal ecological intervention

**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

A chemical may be legally registered for defined uses and still not be the best intervention for a particular ecosystem.

Victorian waterway guidance explicitly treats waterway management as a whole-system problem involving:

- whether intervention is necessary;
- target weeds and ecological condition;
- off-target effects;
- native vegetation;
- revegetation;
- monitoring;
- repeated-treatment requirements;
- integrated management.

The original objective is therefore better expressed as:

> **Improve or protect river-system health**

rather than:

> **Kill weeds**.

`weed killed` may be a useful local metric.

It may not silently become the objective.

---

## Finding A8 — The exact originating riverbank operation cannot yet be adjudicated

**Status:** `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`

The following were not retrieved:

- council and responsible waterway authority;
- exact river reach;
- target weed species;
- contractor/work order;
- product/formulation;
- concentration and application rate;
- label instructions applying to that product/site;
- weather/wind conditions;
- distance from open water;
- public-access/signage arrangements;
- water/sediment/residue monitoring.

Therefore OGI cannot presently determine whether the observed operation was:

- label-compliant;
- environmentally necessary;
- proportionate;
- excessive;
- unsafe;
- or inferior to a feasible alternative.

Any categorical conclusion on the local operation would outrun the evidence.

---

## Finding A9 — APVMA has a documented industry-funded cost-recovery structure

**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

APVMA states that it is primarily funded on a cost-recovery basis by the agricultural and veterinary chemical industry it regulates, through fees, charges and levies. The funding model is changing from 2027–28, including a reduction in sales-based levies and greater upfront fee recovery.

Australian Government reviews have also documented serious regulator-wide governance problems and allegations of industry capture during the period reviewed.

These facts belong in the field ledger.

### Corruption claim

> `APVMA's glyphosate decision is corrupt because industry funds the regulator.`

**Status:** `NOT ESTABLISHED`

No glyphosate-specific causal chain demonstrating a bought or corrupted decision was retrieved.

Funding provenance raises a legitimate structural-conflict question.

It is not itself a truth score.

---

## Finding A10 — Commercial and political incentive relationships are real, but must not be inflated

**Status:** `SUPPORTED WITH LIMITS`

Bayer reported substantial revenue from glyphosate-based products, and Australian Electoral Commission records show disclosed political donations by Bayer Australia.

Those are documented incentive-graph edges.

They do not establish:

- quid pro quo;
- hidden political influence;
- manipulated glyphosate regulation;
- or falsity of Bayer-supported science.

OGI records the relation and then asks what evidence shows the consequence.

---

## Finding A11 — Scientific provenance failures can materially alter confidence

**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

A widely cited 2000 Roundup safety review was formally retracted in 2025 after concerns about authorship, sponsor involvement/conflicts and evidence selection.

A 2026 publication disputes the retraction.

OGI preserves both the correction and its challenge.

The event demonstrates why provenance and correction history matter.

It does not permit the system to infer that every industry-linked glyphosate study is false.

---

# A.7 Alternative-Path Audit

The case began with:

> **There are weeds beside the river.**

The first intervention path was:

> herbicide → weed dies.

OGI reopened the higher-level objective:

> **What intervention produces the healthiest viable river system with the least necessary disruption?**

Candidate pathways include:

1. repeated broad or targeted herbicide treatment;
2. manual/mechanical removal;
3. cut-and-paint or other highly targeted application where appropriate;
4. revegetation and competitive native establishment;
5. integrated combinations;
6. low/no intervention where the purported weed problem does not justify intervention.

The case does not yet contain enough site-specific evidence to rank these universally.

The important OGI result is architectural:

> **Permission to use an intervention is not proof that it is the best path to the original objective.**

For a restorative ecological task, a useful additional metric is whether successful management reduces repeated external intervention over time.

# A.8 Subject finding

## `MIXED`

The first OGI v0.3 run supports the following provisional synthesis:

> Glyphosate is an intentionally phytotoxic chemical. Current evidence supports non-target biological effects under some studied conditions, and some glyphosate-based formulations present materially greater aquatic toxicity than glyphosate alone. Australian pet dogs are demonstrably exposed to glyphosate, but the sources and long-term canine health significance remain incompletely established. Human carcinogenicity remains contested across hazard assessment, epidemiology, animal evidence and regulatory risk assessment, with important 2025 evidence currently under formal reassessment. The exact glyphosate product and conditions at the originating riverbank were not retrieved, so the local operation cannot presently be adjudicated as safe, harmful, necessary or optimal. Victorian waterway guidance supports evaluating intervention necessity, alternatives, off-target effects, revegetation and whole-system outcome rather than reducing success to weed death alone.

This finding is provisional and explicitly reopenable.

# A.9 What remains unknown

The strongest research-debt items are:

1. **Local records** — exactly what was sprayed, by whom, where, why and under what conditions?
2. **Dog exposure pathways** — how are Australian dogs acquiring glyphosate, and what are the long-term canine consequences?
3. **Whole-system comparison** — over multiple years, how do chemical, mechanical, revegetation and integrated approaches compare at the actual riverbank?
4. **Regulatory reopening** — what will ECHA/EFSA and US EPA conclude after considering the newer evidence?
5. **Evidence-production asymmetry** — what proportion of the full corpus is industry-funded, independent, proprietary, inaccessible, replicated or never funded?
6. **Wider corpus** — what changes after a preregistered systematic search of non-English and local databases?

OGI does not convert these missing experiments into evidence for either side.

# A.11 Corrections are evidence

The precursor conversation contained mistakes and overreach.

Examples include:

- language that slid too quickly from dog exposure to `poisoning`;
- premature emphasis on absorption directly through paw pads;
- initial attempts to reassure by comparing dog exposure with a human ADI;
- incomplete early separation between glyphosate and glyphosate-based formulations.

v0.3 did not delete those failures.

They helped produce the Claim-Type Controller, research-debt rules and dual-finding architecture.

A system that erases the error after correction destroys part of the derivation chain.

# A.12 What Case 001 taught OGI

Case 001 supports the need for the v0.3 additions, particularly:

- **Outcome-Blind Case Object** — user conviction must not become the benchmark;
- **Corpus Formation Protocol** — where the AI searches shapes what it can find;
- **Institutional Provenance** — evidence is produced inside economic and institutional fields;
- **Funding Is Not a Truth Score** — provenance must inform without substituting for method;
- **Claim-Type Controller** — detection, exposure, effect, harm, causation and policy are different claims;
- **Research Debt** — missing experiments are not evidence for either side;
- **Alternative-Path Audit** — a permitted intervention may still be the wrong solution;
- **Whole-System Outcome Audit** — local KPI success may defeat the actual system objective;
- **Dual Finding Protocol** — a persuasive case result cannot validate the architecture that produced it.

The case therefore did not answer OGI's research problem by producing certainty.

It produced something more useful:

> **A traceable state that knows what it supports, what it does not support, what it has not yet inspected, and what would make it change.**

That is closer to the research partner OGI was designed to become.

## Appendix A conclusion

The first complex OGI case did **not** return the seductive answer:

> glyphosate safe

or

> glyphosate poison.

It returned a structured mess.

That is a compliment.

The system separated claims, retained contrary evidence, preserved unknowns, exposed missing local facts, traced incentives without converting them into guilt, reopened the original ecological objective, and recorded its own corrections.

At the same time, it failed the strongest possible version of its own test because v0.3 is not yet fully executable.

So the provisional conclusion of Appendix A is:

> **The OGI method shows promise as a rigorous research-partner architecture under contested real-world inquiry, but the current result remains a manually executed demonstration of the specification rather than proof that an implemented OGI system can reliably reproduce the behaviour.**

That is exactly why the next stage is implementation and rerun.
