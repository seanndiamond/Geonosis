# COP-OGI-001 Evidence Ledger

**Case:** Glyphosate and Victorian riverbank weed-management field  
**Purpose:** OGI v0.3 adversarial inquiry stress test  
**Desired finding:** `NONE`  
**Legal standing:** `NONE`  
**Status:** provisional, reopenable

This ledger records what each source can support. It is not a vote count and it does not convert funding, prestige, jurisdiction, publication status, or regulatory authority into a truth score.

## Regulatory and hazard evidence

### E-001 — IARC Monograph 112
- **Source:** International Agency for Research on Cancer, 2015.
- **URL:** https://www.iarc.who.int/featured--news/media-centre-iarc-news-glyphosate/
- **Finding used:** IARC classifies glyphosate as Group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans, based on limited human evidence, sufficient experimental-animal evidence, and strong genotoxicity evidence.
- **Claim fit:** `HAZARD_CLASSIFICATION`; informs `POPULATION_RISK_SUPPORTED` only indirectly.
- **Does not establish:** the risk from every real-world use or exposure level.

### E-002 — APVMA current glyphosate position
- **Source:** Australian Pesticides and Veterinary Medicines Authority.
- **URL:** https://www.apvma.gov.au/resources/frequently-searched-chemicals/glyphosate
- **Finding used:** glyphosate remains registered in Australia; APVMA states it is not aware of evidence warranting reconsideration and says its prior assessments found no reliable evidence that registered glyphosate products cause cancer in humans when used as directed.
- **Claim fit:** regulatory `RISK_ASSESSMENT` and current policy state.
- **Does not establish:** zero hazard, zero effect, or optimality of any particular riverbank use.

### E-003 — APVMA safety methodology
- **Source:** APVMA.
- **URL:** https://www.apvma.gov.au/resources/frequently-searched-chemicals/glyphosate/how-does-apvma-determine-agricultural-chemical-safe-use-australia
- **Finding used:** APVMA distinguishes hazard from risk and states that registration is based on expected exposure under approved use. APVMA says it considers published studies and the whole product.
- **Additional fact:** APVMA says that as of January 2025 its scientists had considered 5,425 glyphosate publications.
- **Claim fit:** regulatory process provenance.
- **Does not establish:** that every publication is equally deeply inspected or that all relevant proprietary data are publicly reproducible.

### E-004 — Australian glyphosate ADI
- **Source:** APVMA Acceptable Daily Intakes, Edition 4.
- **URL:** https://www.apvma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-12/Acceptable%20daily%20intakes%20%28ADI%29%20for%20agricultural%20and%20veterinary%20chemicals%20%E2%80%93%20Edition%204%2C%20December%202023.pdf
- **Finding used:** Australian glyphosate ADI is 0.3 mg/kg body weight/day, based on a 1985 three-generation rat reproduction study with a NOAEL of 30 mg/kg/day.
- **Claim fit:** regulatory exposure benchmark provenance.
- **Does not establish:** a dog-specific safe level or zero biological effect below the ADI.

### E-005 — APVMA waterway review
- **Source:** APVMA glyphosate chemical review.
- **URL:** https://www.apvma.gov.au/chemicals-and-products/chemical-review/listing/glyphosate
- **Finding used:** APVMA found glyphosate active constituent low risk in its 1996 review but found undesirably high aquatic toxicity for some formulations because of surfactants; restrictions and label changes followed.
- **Claim fit:** `ADVERSE_EFFECT_SUPPORTED` for some formulations/aquatic organisms and `POLICY_ACTION_SUPPORTED` historically for restrictions.
- **Does not establish:** that every current formulation or permitted waterway use is unacceptable.

### E-006 — EFSA 2023 peer review
- **Source:** European Food Safety Authority.
- **URL:** https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/news/glyphosate-no-critical-areas-concern-data-gaps-identified
- **Finding used:** EFSA reported no critical area of concern preventing renewal but identified unresolved issues/data gaps, including aquatic plants, an impurity, consumer dietary assessment, and a formulation component.
- **Claim fit:** current EU regulatory risk assessment.
- **Does not establish:** a universal absence of hazard.

### E-007 — European Commission approval
- **Source:** European Commission.
- **URL:** https://food.ec.europa.eu/plants/pesticides/approval-active-substances-safeners-and-synergists/renewal-approval/glyphosate_en
- **Finding used:** glyphosate is approved as an active substance in the EU until 15 December 2033 subject to conditions/restrictions and national product authorisations.
- **Claim fit:** regulatory status.
- **Does not establish:** unrestricted use or finality of the scientific state.

### E-008 — ECHA 2026 reassessment state
- **Source:** European Chemicals Agency.
- **URL:** https://echa.europa.eu/hot-topics/glyphosate
- **Finding used:** ECHA's Risk Assessment Committee began reassessing the 2025 Global Glyphosate Study in 2026 at the European Commission's request.
- **Claim fit:** `REOPEN_TRIGGER` / current-state update.
- **Does not establish:** what the reassessment will conclude.

### E-009 — US EPA current state
- **Source:** US Environmental Protection Agency.
- **URL:** https://www.epa.gov/ingredients-used-pesticide-products/glyphosate
- **Finding used:** EPA is actively reevaluating glyphosate and released a new open-literature search in August 2026; updated human-health/carcinogenicity assessment is expected in late 2026.
- **Claim fit:** current regulatory state and `REOPEN_TRIGGER`.
- **Does not establish:** the outcome of the pending review.

## Animal, environmental and formulation evidence

### E-010 — Australian pet-dog biomonitoring
- **Source:** Liu et al., Environmental Toxicology and Chemistry, 2026.
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/etc/advance-article/doi/10.1093/etojnl/vgag142/8723134
- **Finding used:** glyphosate was detected in 68% of 71 Australian pet-dog urine samples; AMPA in 13%; maximum glyphosate concentration about 49 µg/L. Authors report no dog-specific ADI and call for toxicokinetic/exposure-pathway work.
- **Funding:** Australian Research Council Linkage Project; Queensland Health support acknowledged; authors declared no conflict of interest.
- **Claim fit:** `DETECTED` and `EXPOSURE_SUPPORTED`.
- **Does not establish:** source of exposure, paw absorption as the dominant route, clinical poisoning, lifetime disease, or harm from the originating riverbank.

### E-011 — 2024 animal-toxicity meta-analysis
- **Source:** Evalen et al., Environmental Pollution, 2024.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38460584/
- **Finding used:** meta-analysis of 1,282 observations from 121 articles concluded glyphosate/glyphosate-based herbicides are generally sublethally toxic to animals, particularly aquatic/marine animals; publication bias was detected.
- **Claim fit:** `BIOLOGICAL_EFFECT_SUPPORTED` / some `ADVERSE_EFFECT_SUPPORTED` depending on endpoint.
- **Limitation:** broad heterogeneous animal corpus; publication bias cautions against simple effect-size extrapolation.

### E-012 — 2026 aquatic meta-analysis
- **Source:** integrated review/meta-analysis of aquatic ecotoxicity and fate, 2026.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41831893/
- **Finding used:** identifies chronic aquatic hazard thresholds, non-target biological effects and greater toxicity of commercial formulations than glyphosate alone; identifies low-level chronic exposure and mixtures as knowledge gaps.
- **Claim fit:** `BIOLOGICAL_EFFECT_SUPPORTED` and `ADVERSE_EFFECT_SUPPORTED` for aquatic taxa under studied exposures.
- **Does not establish:** that concentrations at the originating riverbank exceed relevant thresholds.

### E-013 — 2021 ecotoxicology review reaching lower-risk conclusion
- **Source:** Rodríguez-Gil et al., 2021.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34104986/
- **Finding used:** review concludes glyphosate generally has low environmental persistence/bioaccumulation risk under good agricultural practice and that some POEA-containing formulations are substantially more toxic than glyphosate alone.
- **Claim fit:** materially contrary environmental interpretation.
- **Use in case:** retained to prevent one-sided corpus construction.

### E-014 — Environmental fate factsheet
- **Source:** US EPA technical factsheet.
- **URL:** https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1012HTU.TXT
- **Finding used:** glyphosate sorbs strongly to soil/sediment; average soil half-life reported around 60 days in the cited factsheet; water-column dissipation can be faster; sediment can become a sink; low bioconcentration expected.
- **Claim fit:** environmental fate.
- **Does not establish:** identical persistence in every soil, river or formulation.

### E-015 — 2025 Global Glyphosate Study, carcinogenicity arm
- **Source:** Panzacchi et al., Environmental Health, 2025.
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12940-025-01187-2
- **Finding used:** 1,020 Sprague-Dawley rats exposed from prenatal life through 104 weeks; authors report statistically significant tumour increases in glyphosate and two glyphosate-based-herbicide groups, including at the EU ADI-equivalent dose.
- **Funding:** Ramazzini Institute and multiple listed foundations/research organisations; no glyphosate-manufacturer funding listed in the inspected funding statement.
- **Claim fit:** `ADVERSE_EFFECT_SUPPORTED` / animal carcinogenicity evidence.
- **Does not establish:** human cancer causation by itself.
- **Current state:** formally under ECHA/EFSA reassessment.

## Human epidemiology and biomonitoring

### E-016 — Agricultural Health Study, 2018 update
- **Source:** Andreotti et al., Journal of the National Cancer Institute.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29136183/
- **Finding used:** prospective cohort of 54,251 pesticide applicators found no statistically significant association between glyphosate and solid tumours or lymphoid malignancies overall, including NHL; an AML signal at highest exposure warranted confirmation.
- **Claim fit:** materially contrary epidemiological evidence for general cancer/NHL claims.

### E-017 — 2019 NHL meta-analysis
- **Source:** Zhang et al., Mutation Research Reviews.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31342895/
- **Finding used:** meta-analysis focusing on highest exposure groups reported a 41% increased meta-relative risk of NHL.
- **Funding:** PubMed lists NIEHS/NIH training support.
- **Claim fit:** `POPULATION_RISK_SUPPORTED` for an association in high-exposure groups, not individual causation.

### E-018 — 2021 updated NHL meta-analysis
- **Source:** Boffetta et al., 2021.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34142676/
- **Finding used:** meta-RR for ever-exposure was 1.05 (95% CI 0.90–1.24); concluded lack of association with NHL overall while an association with DLBCL could not be ruled out.
- **Claim fit:** materially contrary meta-analytic interpretation.

### E-019 — Mexico child biomonitoring
- **Source:** seasonal urinary glyphosate study, Jalisco, Mexico.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34759787/
- **Finding used:** glyphosate detected in all 95 sampled children; levels varied by season/age; study did not establish glyphosate-related clinical toxicity.
- **Claim fit:** `DETECTED` and `EXPOSURE_SUPPORTED`.

### E-020 — China child kidney-biomarker study
- **Source:** Liu et al., Science of the Total Environment, 2024.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38007119/
- **Finding used:** glyphosate detected in 92.05% of 239 children's urine samples; urinary glyphosate positively associated with two renal injury biomarkers. Authors call for larger epidemiology.
- **Claim fit:** `EXPOSURE_SUPPORTED` plus biomarker association.
- **Does not establish:** clinical kidney disease causation.

### E-021 — Argentina breast-cancer case-control study
- **Source:** Doná et al., 2025.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40469091/
- **Finding used:** glyphosate detected in 86.1% of sampled women; urinary levels did not differ between case/control groups, while residential proximity to agricultural fields was associated with higher breast-cancer odds.
- **Claim fit:** strong reminder not to collapse mixed agrochemical-field exposure into a glyphosate-specific causal claim.

### E-022 — Argentina rural pesticide exposure/biomarkers
- **Source:** Santa Fe rural-population study, 2026.
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41797357/
- **Finding used:** pesticide residues detected in 62% of blood samples, more frequent in rural participants, with increased oxidative/genotoxic biomarkers; glyphosate/AMPA found in 67% of sampled soils.
- **Claim fit:** pesticide-field exposure and biological-response evidence.
- **Does not establish:** glyphosate alone caused the measured human biomarker changes.

## River-management and alternative-path evidence

### E-023 — Victorian Technical Guidelines for Waterway Management
- **Source:** Victorian Government, 2024 technical guidelines.
- **URL:** https://www.water.vic.gov.au/waterways/technical-guidelines-for-waterway-management
- **Accessible PDF:** https://www.water.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0015/722301/technical-guidelines-for-waterway-management-accessible.pdf
- **Finding used:** waterway management should begin by determining whether intervention is necessary; guidance emphasises native vegetation, monitoring, revegetation, weed-management planning, off-target damage and repeated-treatment assessment.
- **Claim fit:** `POLICY_ACTION_SUPPORTED` for an integrated, monitored, whole-system management approach.
- **Does not establish:** that manual removal is always superior to targeted herbicide.

### E-024 — Victorian EPA general environmental duty
- **Source:** EPA Victoria.
- **URL:** https://www.epa.vic.gov.au/reasonably-practicable
- **Finding used:** risks of harm should be eliminated or reduced so far as reasonably practicable, considering likelihood, degree of harm, knowledge, available/suitable risk-reduction methods and cost; cumulative impacts count as harm.
- **Claim fit:** decision framework relevant to alternative-path audit.

## Institutional and incentive-field evidence

### E-025 — APVMA funding structure
- **Source:** APVMA Corporate Plan 2026–30 / cost recovery.
- **URLs:**
  - https://www.apvma.gov.au/news-forms-and-publications/publications/corporate-plan/26-30
  - https://www.apvma.gov.au/about/about-us/apvma-cost-recovery-arrangements
- **Finding used:** APVMA is primarily funded on a cost-recovery basis by the industry it regulates; fees/charges and sales-based levies contribute to funding. A revised funding model from 2027–28 reduces sales-based levies and increases direct fee recovery.
- **Claim fit:** `FIELD_PROVENANCE`.
- **Does not establish:** that any glyphosate decision is corrupt or scientifically wrong.

### E-029 — 2000 Roundup safety review retraction
- **Source:** Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology retraction notice, 2025/2026.
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0273230025002387
- **Finding used:** the journal formally retracted the 2000 Williams/Kroes/Munro review citing concerns about authorship, sponsor contribution/conflicts and evidence selection.
- **Claim fit:** evidence-production provenance and correction history.

- **Finding used:** Roundup Ready crops are genetically engineered for glyphosate tolerance, enabling crop survival during labelled glyphosate applications.
- **Claim fit:** commercial/agronomic system architecture.
- **Does not establish:** that GM food is unsafe or rejected as food by animals.

## Evidence not promoted

The following propositions were deliberately **not** promoted to findings in this run:

- that glyphosate at the originating site definitely entered the dog through paw pads;
- that detection of glyphosate in urine equals poisoning;
- that glyphosate necessarily bioaccumulates up aquatic food chains;
- that every glyphosate use causes cancer;
- that regulatory approval proves zero harm;
- that APVMA's funding model proves a glyphosate-specific corrupt decision;
- that industry-funded research is false by definition;
- that independently funded research is true by definition;
- that a video of animals preferring non-GM corn proves GM corn is not food;
- that manual weed removal is always ecologically superior to targeted chemical use.

Those boundaries are part of the OGI test result.
