## Search S2 — Procedure and contestability
**Question:** Did Armstrong have a path to contest the charges?

**Sources:** USADA reasoned decision and case chronology.

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

Armstrong challenged USADA's jurisdiction in federal court, lost that challenge, then chose not to proceed to arbitration before neutral arbitrators. USADA imposed the sanction after he declined the arbitration route.

Important boundary: refusal to arbitrate is not itself proof of doping. It matters to whether the institutional process offered a contest path.

## Search S3 — UCI and WADA response
**Question:** Did the international sporting bodies displace or accept the USADA result?

**Sources:** USADA chronology; WADA Executive Committee records; UCI later institutional records.

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

UCI accepted and enforced the sanction; WADA did not appeal.

## Search S4 — Armstrong's later admission
**Question:** Did Armstrong later admit the central conduct?

**Source:** ABC transcript/excerpt of January 2013 Oprah Winfrey interview.

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

Armstrong answered yes to use of banned substances, EPO, blood transfusions and other prohibited substances, and yes to doping during all seven Tour de France victories.

## Search S5 — Historical institutional reliability
**Question:** Was UCI's anti-doping conduct during the Armstrong era uniformly reliable?

**Sources:** UCI Cycling Independent Reform Commission report and linked publication materials.

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

The CIRC inquiry documented serious historical governance and anti-doping failures, while rejecting some specific corruption allegations for lack of sufficient evidence.
