# COP-OGI-005 — Lance Armstrong and When the Institution Earns the Finding

**OGI version:** v0.3

**Subject finding:** `SUPPORTED`  
**OGI performance:** `MIXED`  
**Reopenable:** yes

This case tests whether OGI can recognize strong institutional evidence when the institution actually exposes its derivation, while still refusing to treat institutional status itself as proof.

The originating social dispute was simple:

- one observer suspected Lance Armstrong was doping;
- a knowledgeable cycling enthusiast believed Armstrong's extraordinary training and ability could explain his success;
- later anti-doping institutions produced a very large evidentiary record.

The central question is not who won the pub argument. It is:

> **When does an institutional finding deserve to become the current warranted state?**

Current result: USADA's 2012 finding is strongly supported by a public evidentiary chain, multiple sworn witnesses, documentary and scientific evidence, procedural opportunity to contest, adoption by international sporting bodies, and Armstrong's later direct admission that he used prohibited performance-enhancing methods during all seven Tour de France victories.

The case also preserves an important correction: later confirmation does not retroactively make an earlier intuition well-derived if the original evidence was weak or incomplete.
