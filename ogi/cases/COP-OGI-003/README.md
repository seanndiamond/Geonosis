> **The passenger decision is not examined in isolation. The decision field is.**

This case asks how a paying participant could decide to enter the OceanGate Titan submersible for a dive to the Titanic wreck, and where the failure in that decision process arose.

The case does not attempt to reconstruct the private mental state of any deceased individual without direct evidence. It reconstructs the publicly evidenced information field surrounding the decision.

## Current subject state

The evidence supports that the passenger decision occurred inside a distorted information field containing both explicit risk warnings and strong trust/safety proxies.

Relevant features include:

- OceanGate explicitly disclosed that Titan was experimental and uncertified;
- the waiver repeatedly warned of death and catastrophic failure;
- OceanGate simultaneously marketed the expedition as a professional exploration program and its paying passengers as "mission specialists";
- Titan had completed previous Titanic dives, creating apparent operational proof;
- the founder personally piloted Titan and publicly expressed confidence in its design;
- OceanGate promoted safety systems, procedures, prior operations, partnerships, and expedition legitimacy;
- independent classification was absent;
- critical warnings, contrary technical views, regulatory concerns, and hull-anomaly evidence existed outside the ordinary passenger's direct inspection field;
- later U.S. Coast Guard and Transportation Safety Board investigations found major failures in design, testing, certification/oversight, organizational safety culture, risk management, and response to contradictory evidence.

The case does **not** establish the exact subjective reasoning of any one passenger.

## Provisional OGI finding

The strongest explanation is not "the passenger ignored danger" and not "the passenger was deceived by one lie." The decision emerged from **proxy stacking under information asymmetry**: confidence, prior success, founder participation, expert presence, professional expedition framing, safety procedures, price and exclusivity could collectively substitute for independent engineering verification.

The waiver did provide a genuine contrary signal. A passenger therefore retained some responsibility for accepting an explicitly experimental and uncertified activity. But the waiver did not give the passenger equal access to the underlying engineering state, ignored hull anomalies, failed oversight, rejected expert objections, or the organizational processes that had constructed safety internally.

The case therefore separates:

- **risk disclosure** from **risk comprehension**;
- **operator confidence** from **independent warrant**;
- **previous survival** from **demonstrated structural safety**;
- **consent to danger** from **informed access to the true state of the danger**.

## Candidate OGI lesson

`TRUST_PROXY_STACKING`

> Multiple individually weak signs of legitimacy can combine into a powerful apparent safety signal and displace the question that actually matters: what independent evidence establishes the safety of the system?

This remains a candidate lesson, not a constitutional invariant.
