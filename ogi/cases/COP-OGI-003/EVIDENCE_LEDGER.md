# COP-OGI-003 Evidence Ledger

## E-001 — The activity was explicitly described as experimental and uncertified
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

The OceanGate waiver stated that the experimental submersible had not been approved or certified by any regulatory body and warned that failure could cause injury or death. The USCG final report states the waiver mentioned death nine times.

**Boundary:** This establishes disclosed category risk. It does not establish that a passenger knew the detailed engineering state or organizational history.

## E-002 — Titan lacked independent classification / regulatory oversight
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

USCG found Titan unregistered, unclassed and without USCG ORV designation. The 2026 Canadian TSB report likewise found that Titan operated without regulator or classification-society oversight.

## E-003 — Paying passengers were presented as mission specialists
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

USCG found OceanGate misrepresented paying passengers as mission specialists and used that structure in its operating/regulatory model. TSB refers to the three non-crew occupants of the fatal dive as passengers whom OceanGate called mission specialists.

## E-004 — OceanGate created strong public legitimacy signals
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

USCG documents extensive marketing, social media, influencer outreach, public speaking, newsletters and expedition framing. Contemporaneous reporting showed checklists, multiple return systems, founder confidence and prior operational experience.

**Boundary:** These are legitimacy/trust signals, not engineering proof.

## E-005 — Prior successful dives did not establish remaining structural safety
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

The 2026 TSB found progressive carbon-fibre hull damage accumulated during repeated dives and that OceanGate did not know how long the hull would remain safe under repeated Titanic-depth cycling.

## E-006 — OceanGate possessed important contradictory safety information
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

USCG found that 2022 RTM and acoustic data showed significant changes in hull behaviour that were not adequately analyzed or acted upon. External and internal experts had also raised concerns over design, testing and classification.

## E-007 — OceanGate's internal risk-construction process was compromised
**Status:** `SUPPORTED`

TSB found risk management was hindered by organizational structure, power dynamics, groupthink and confirmation bias. Expert input was marginalized and independent challenge was absent. USCG separately found a toxic workplace culture and critically flawed safety practices.

## E-008 — The passenger's exact private reasoning is knowable from the public record
**Status:** `UNSUPPORTED`

The case has not retrieved sufficient contemporaneous personal evidence to say precisely why any named passenger chose to board.

## E-009 — The passenger bears zero responsibility because the operator field was defective
**Status:** `UNSUPPORTED`

The explicit waiver and experimental/uncertified status were meaningful warnings. Choosing to proceed despite them remains relevant to personal decision analysis.

## E-010 — The passenger alone caused the bad decision
**Status:** `UNSUPPORTED`

The available evidence shows material information asymmetry, absent independent oversight, misleading passenger framing, stacked trust proxies, ignored contradictory evidence and a defective organizational risk process.

## Provisional synthesis

The decision was not a single error. It was a **distributed decision failure**:

`adventure objective + explicit danger warning + strong legitimacy proxies + asymmetric technical information + prior-success normalization + defective internal risk process + missing external oversight -> passenger proceeds`

The critical OGI question is not whether the passenger saw a warning. It is whether the field allowed the passenger to distinguish **operator confidence** from **independent engineering warrant**.
