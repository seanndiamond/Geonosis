# Appendix C — Titan and the Decision Field

## Methodological declaration

This appendix is a stress test of OGI v0.3.

- case: `COP-OGI-003`
- subject: decision field surrounding a paying passenger entering the OceanGate Titan submersible
- purpose: test whether OGI can reconstruct how a catastrophic personal decision emerged without collapsing the event into victim blame or operator blame alone
- desired finding: `NONE`
- legal standing: `NONE`
- reopenable: yes

The originating question was:

> A few years ago, a person made a decision. They climbed into a submersible to visit the Titanic wreck as a paying tourist, and it cost them their life. How did the mistake arrive for the person to climb into that device?

## 1. What OGI may and may not infer

OGI cannot know the exact private reasoning of a deceased passenger without direct contemporaneous evidence from that person.

It can reconstruct:

- what risks OceanGate disclosed;
- what safety and legitimacy signals OceanGate presented;
- what independent engineering and regulatory safeguards were absent;
- what contrary safety information existed;
- what the operator itself knew or should have known;
- what later investigations established about the actual technical and organizational field.

The object of inquiry is therefore **decision-in-field**.

## 2. The passenger was not told that there was no risk

OceanGate's liability waiver was unusually explicit. The U.S. Coast Guard found that the waiver mentioned death nine times and stated that Titan was experimental, had not been certified by any regulatory body, would be exposed to extreme pressure, and could cause severe injury or death. The waiver allowed participants to decline a dive.

This matters.

A complete analysis cannot say the passenger was simply told the activity was safe.

There was an explicit warning signal in the field.

However, the Coast Guard also found that videos showed some 2023 mission specialists signing the waiver while already aboard the support vessel en route to the Titanic site, and it was unknown whether they had received it earlier.

Source:
- U.S. Coast Guard Marine Board of Investigation, *Report of the Marine Board of Investigation into the Implosion of the Submersible Titan*, 2025.
- OceanGate Expeditions Waiver and Release of Liability, USCG exhibit CG-035.

## 3. The same field contained powerful legitimacy signals

OceanGate did not present the experience as random thrill-seeking.

The Coast Guard found that OceanGate used a comprehensive marketing strategy involving social media, media relations, influencers, newsletters, public displays and speaking engagements. The target market included ultra-high-net-worth adventure explorers.

Paying passengers were called **mission specialists** and were presented as members of an expedition team rather than ordinary tourists. The Coast Guard later found that this status was misrepresented and used in part to conceal what were in substance paying passenger operations.

The expedition also contained powerful social proof:

- Titan had previously reached Titanic depth;
- OceanGate's founder personally piloted the vehicle;
- an internationally experienced Titanic/deep-sea expert participated in the fatal dive;
- OceanGate publicly promoted multiple return systems, procedures and real-time hull monitoring;
- major-media access presented the program as a real operating expedition rather than an obviously reckless stunt.

These cues do not prove safety. But to a non-specialist they can function as **trust proxies**.

Sources:
- U.S. Coast Guard MBI report, sections on OceanGate marketing, mission specialists and regulatory compliance.
- Transportation Safety Board of Canada, M23A0169, 2026.
- CBS News reporting from the 2022 OceanGate expedition.

## 4. The engineering state was materially worse than the visible trust proxies implied

The U.S. Coast Guard concluded in 2025 that the deaths were preventable and identified OceanGate's inadequate design, certification, maintenance and inspection processes as primary contributing factors.

The report found that Titan was unregistered, unclassed and lacked independent regulatory oversight. It also concluded that OceanGate progressively moved away from established oversight and misrepresented paying passengers as mission specialists.

The Coast Guard further found that OceanGate had data from its real-time monitoring system showing significant changes in hull behaviour after the 2022 season, including increased acoustic activity, but failed to conduct substantial analysis, obtain competent independent analysis, or take appropriate preventative action.

The Transportation Safety Board of Canada independently concluded in 2026 that Titan's carbon-fibre hull accumulated damage during repeated dives, that its as-built properties were never adequately tested against the theoretical design assumptions, and that OceanGate did not know how long the hull would remain safe under repeated Titanic-depth cycling.

Sources:
- USCG MBI report, 2025.
- TSB Canada M23A0169, 2026.

## 5. The operator's own risk field was corrupted before the passenger ever arrived

This is central to the OGI analysis.

The Canadian TSB found that OceanGate's risk management was affected by company structure, power dynamics, groupthink and confirmation bias. Subject-matter experts who raised contrary perspectives were marginalized, external advice that challenged the preferred approach was often rejected, and the CEO's worldview had outsized influence over how risk was constructed inside the company.

The TSB explicitly found that OceanGate's risk management did not identify and mitigate key risks to Titan's structural integrity.

The U.S. Coast Guard similarly identified a toxic workplace culture, poor governance, inadequate certification/inspection, ineffective whistleblower pathways and a pattern of disregarding established safety protocols.

Thus, the passenger was not merely making a decision in the presence of risk.

They were making a decision using safety signals generated by an organization whose **own internal process for deciding what was safe had already failed**.

## 6. Prior successful dives were not the proof they appeared to be

Titan had previously descended to Titanic depth.

For a passenger this can look like empirical evidence:

> It has done this before. Therefore the engineering works.

But a repeatedly cycled pressure hull is not necessarily validated by survival of previous cycles. The later Canadian investigation found progressive damage accumulated through repeated dives.

Therefore prior success may have operated as a dangerous proxy:

`previous survival -> assumed future safety`

The correct engineering question was:

> What evidence establishes remaining structural life after repeated pressure cycles?

OceanGate did not have a sufficiently warranted answer.

## 7. The waiver created a paradox rather than resolving the field

One might say:

> The passenger signed a waiver that said experimental, uncertified, and death. The decision failure is therefore entirely theirs.

OGI does not accept that compression.

The waiver demonstrates that the passenger accepted a **category of catastrophic risk**.

It does not establish that the passenger knew:

- the pressure hull had accumulated damage;
- critical 2022 monitoring anomalies had not been properly analyzed;
- independent classification was absent after established industry concern;
- contrary technical voices had been marginalized;
- the company's risk process suffered from groupthink and confirmation bias;
- regulatory gaps were being actively navigated rather than independent safety approval obtained.

This distinction is fundamental:

> **Consent to danger is not identical to informed access to the actual state of the danger.**

## 8. Where did the passenger's decision fail?

OGI's provisional answer is distributed across the field.

### Individual decision vulnerability

The paying participant accepted an explicitly experimental, uncertified, potentially fatal activity without requiring independent engineering certification or an equivalent external safety warrant.

That is a real decision-level vulnerability.

### Information asymmetry

The operator possessed or had access to technical and organizational information that a passenger could not reasonably inspect directly.

### Trust-proxy substitution

Signals such as founder confidence, expert participation, previous dives, expedition language, price, exclusivity, procedures and apparent technical sophistication could substitute psychologically for independent evidence.

### Organizational risk-construction failure

OceanGate's own process for determining what was safe was later found to have been compromised by power concentration, groupthink, confirmation bias and rejection/marginalization of contrary expert input.

### Regulatory-field failure

Titan operated without the type of external classification and regulatory oversight that could have interrupted the company's internal risk narrative.

### Temporal normalization

Each previous successful dive could make the next dive feel safer even while cumulative pressure-cycle damage was increasing.

## 9. Current subject finding

### Claim A — The passenger had no warning of serious risk.
`UNSUPPORTED`

The waiver explicitly disclosed experimental status, lack of certification and possibility of death.

### Claim B — The passenger had full information about Titan's actual engineering risk state.
`UNSUPPORTED`

The later investigations reveal critical technical, organizational and regulatory facts that were not reducible to the waiver's generic danger language.

### Claim C — Previous successful dives demonstrated Titan was structurally safe for the fatal dive.
`UNSUPPORTED`

Later investigation found progressive hull damage and inadequate lifecycle knowledge.

### Claim D — OceanGate's internal safety assessment was reliable.
`UNSUPPORTED`

Both the USCG and TSB identified major failures in risk management, engineering practice and oversight.

### Claim E — The passenger alone caused the bad decision.
`UNSUPPORTED`

The choice occurred inside a materially distorted information and authority field.

### Claim F — The passenger had no responsibility for accepting the risk.
`UNSUPPORTED`

The explicit waiver and uncertified experimental status were meaningful contrary signals.

### Overall subject state
`MIXED`

The fatal choice is best understood as a personal decision made inside a defective safety-information field, not as either pure victim error or pure operator coercion.

## 10. OGI lesson

This case suggests a candidate failure mode:

### `TRUST_PROXY_STACKING`

> Multiple weak or indirect indicators of legitimacy can combine to overpower a single direct warning, even when none of the proxies independently establishes the proposition that matters.

In this case:

`founder confidence + expert presence + prior dives + mission framing + professional procedures + high price + apparent sophistication`

could collectively be perceived as:

`this system has been independently shown safe`

when that proposition was false.

A second candidate principle is:

> **For irreversible high-consequence decisions, the user should ask for the independent warrant, not merely the operator's confidence.**

This remains a case-derived candidate, not yet an OGI invariant.
