# COP-OGI-002 — Court Weight-of-Evidence Finding

**Finding ID:** `COP-OGI-002-WEIGHT-001`  
**Status:** `CURRENT WARRANTED FINDING / REOPENABLE`  
**Legal standing:** `NONE`

## Central propositions and weights

### Spinach contains useful dietary iron
`SUPPORTED — W5 VERY STRONG`

Food-composition data and nutrition guidance support spinach as a legitimate source of nonheme iron.

### Spinach contains vastly more iron than other useful foods or vegetables and should be treated as a special iron remedy
`UNSUPPORTED — W1 WEAK`

Spinach is useful but not uniquely exceptional. Other plant foods can provide comparable or greater iron, and total iron content is not equivalent to absorbed iron.

### A single misplaced decimal point caused the historical spinach-iron belief
`LIMITED — W2`

The popular debunking story is not securely derived from a primary historical chain sufficient to carry the certainty with which it is often repeated.

### Best child-facing advice: spinach is one useful food within a varied iron-containing diet
`SUPPORTED — W5 VERY STRONG`

## Weighted conclusion

> **The Court strongly rejects the childhood claim that spinach is a uniquely extraordinary iron food (`W1`) and very strongly supports the corrective advice that spinach is one useful nonheme-iron food within a varied diet (`W5`). The decimal-point origin story remains only limitedly supported (`W2`) and must not replace the older myth as a new unexamined myth.**

## Reopen triggers

- primary historical evidence resolving the decimal-point claim;
- materially revised food-composition or human-absorption data.

**Court posture:** `SHOW ME.`
