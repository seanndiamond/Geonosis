# COP-OGI-002 Search Ledger

**Date:** 2026-09-06

This ledger records the first search pass. It is not a claim that the entire literature has been exhausted.

## S-001 — Current authoritative nutrition guidance

**Question:** Does spinach contain meaningful dietary iron and how does it compare with other foods?

**Sources searched:**
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements iron fact sheets
- USDA-linked food table through NIH
- Australian Eat for Health / NHMRC iron guidance

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

**Result:** Boiled spinach is listed at about 3 mg iron per 1/2 cup in the NIH table, similar to 1/2 cup boiled lentils and 1/2 cup firm tofu. Australian guidance treats iron requirements at whole-diet level and gives age-specific RDIs.

## S-002 — Bioavailability of spinach iron

**Question:** Is spinach iron readily absorbed?

**Sources searched:**
- NIH iron fact sheet references
- PubMed spinach iron bioavailability studies

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

**Result:** Spinach provides nonheme iron and is described by NIH as having relatively low bioavailability because of absorption-inhibiting compounds. Plant iron absorption is improved by vitamin C and is generally lower than heme iron absorption.

## S-003 — Oxalic acid hypothesis

**Question:** Is spinach iron poorly absorbed mainly because spinach contains oxalic acid?

**Sources searched:**
- PubMed
- European Journal of Clinical Nutrition human stable-isotope study

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

**Result:** A randomized crossover human isotope study found added oxalate did not significantly reduce nonheme iron absorption from kale and concluded oxalic acid in fruits and vegetables is of minor relevance to iron nutrition. The simplistic `oxalate blocks spinach iron` story is therefore not accepted as a sufficient explanation.

## S-004 — Historical decimal-point story

**Question:** Did one misplaced decimal point create the spinach-iron myth?

**Sources searched:**
- academic historical analysis
- Rothamsted repository copy of Mielewczik & Moll (2019)
- academic discussion of the Hamblin/Bender decimal-point story

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

**Result:** The decimal-point story is widely repeated but lacks a clean primary derivation. Historical scholarship finds a more complex nineteenth- and twentieth-century propagation involving science, journalism and popular culture.

## S-005 — Australian child iron requirements

**Question:** What are current Australian iron intake recommendations for children?

**Sources searched:**
- Eat for Health
- NHMRC Nutrient Reference Values

**State:** `SEARCHED_FOUND`

**Result:** Current Australian RDIs include 9 mg/day at ages 1–3, 10 mg/day at ages 4–8, 8 mg/day at ages 9–13, 11 mg/day for boys 14–18 and 15 mg/day for girls 14–18.

## Known gaps

- No exhaustive historical archive search of every nineteenth-century chemical analysis was performed in this run.
- No systematic review of every spinach iron absorption study was performed.
- Current Australian Food Composition Database entries for spinach were not fully reconstructed into serving-level comparisons in this first pass.
- The run did not attempt to diagnose any individual child's iron status.

These are search-scope statements, not evidence against any proposition.
