# Appendix D — The Rabbit-Man and the Impossible Prior

## Methodological declaration

This appendix is a stress test of OGI v0.3.

- case: `COP-OGI-004`
- subject: original intuition rejecting animal-person ontology during the early Saqsayhuamán reading
- purpose: test whether OGI can reopen a strongly held prior without over-promoting later recurrence into literal fact
- desired finding: `NONE`
- legal standing: `NONE`
- reopenable: yes

The originating question is not whether OGI can prove that a literal rabbit-person species existed. It is:

> What should a rigorous research partner do when the investigator's first intuition says an observed possibility is impossible?

## 1. Originating research state

In the Geonosis manuscript lineage, the Saqsayhuamán reading was recorded image-first. The working figure appeared before the name: rabbit-like head, human or near-human body, upright posture, cane, central position, and authority inferred from how the wider field gathered around it.

The early discipline was:

`pattern first -> name second -> implication last`

The figure was initially held as:

- rabbit-man;
- sovereign;
- authority-being;
- possible solar/coherence function.

Only after wider recurrence in the working corpus did `Ra` become the preferred functional name.

At the time of the first encounter, however, the investigator's intuition supplied a stronger prior:

> Animal people cannot exist.

OGI treats that sentence as a proposition requiring its own derivation.

## 2. The first correction: representation is not impossible

The blanket prior immediately fails if its intended meaning is:

> Human-animal composite beings cannot even be valid ancient representations.

Ancient Egyptian material includes documented hare-headed humanoid deities. Academic descriptions of Egyptian funerary material include enthroned hare-headed mummiform gods, and older descriptions of Dendera and Book of the Dead material also record hare-headed divine figures.

Therefore:

`animal-headed humanoid representation exists in ancient material` -> `SUPPORTED`

This does not tell us what such a representation meant, still less whether it portrayed a literal biological species.

Sources inspected:
- Brill, *The Tomb of the Priests of Amun*, description of an enthroned hare-headed mummiform god.
- Historical Egyptian iconographic literature describing hare-headed divinities at Dendera and in Book of the Dead material.

## 3. The second correction: biology is not exhausted by ordinary species breeding

Modern biology also makes the sentence `mixed human-animal biology is impossible` too coarse.

A biological chimera is not the same thing as a sexual hybrid. Human-animal interspecies chimera research uses human pluripotent cells introduced into animal embryos or developmental systems. Reviews document real but limited interspecies chimerism and substantial species barriers that impede integration and development.

This supports:

`limited human-animal cellular chimerism is biologically possible` -> `SUPPORTED`

It does **not** support:

`a naturally breeding adult rabbit-human population is biologically demonstrated` -> `UNSUPPORTED`

or:

`the Saqsayhuamán rabbit-man therefore represents a literal biological person` -> `UNSUPPORTED`

Sources inspected:
- Reviews of human-animal chimera research in PubMed/PMC.
- Reviews of species barriers in interspecies blastocyst complementation.

## 4. What later recurrence changes

The Geonosis project records that the rabbit-man did not remain an isolated reading. Related animal-headed beings, sovereignty/coherence functions, solar/lunar relations, operators and animal-person motifs recurred across the user's wider working corpus.

That recurrence matters epistemically.

It justifies reopening:

`this category is impossible`

It may strengthen:

`there is a recurring representational or functional pattern in the working corpus`

But recurrence alone cannot silently promote:

`recurring image/function`

to:

`literal historical species`

Those are different claims and require different evidence.

## 5. The image-level limitation in this run

The original Saqsayhuamán image set was not independently re-inspected inside this Appendix D run.

The case therefore relies on the preserved Geonosis manuscript record for the history of the observation, while marking direct image-level re-adjudication as:

`KNOWN_BUT_NOT_RETRIEVED / NOT RE-RUN HERE`

This matters because OGI must not use its memory of a prior conclusion as a substitute for showing the image again when the literal visual claim is challenged.

## 6. Two symmetrical failure modes

### Failure mode A — Ontology preemption

The investigator says:

> That kind of being cannot exist, therefore the observation must be wrong.

The prior ontology prevents the observation from receiving a fair hearing.

Candidate label:

`ONTOLOGY_PREEMPTION`

This may be a domain-specific form of the broader premise-lock problem already present in the project method.

### Failure mode B — Recurrence promotion

The investigator later finds similar forms repeatedly and says:

> Therefore the beings literally existed.

The recurrence is asked to establish more than it derives.

Candidate label:

`RECURRENCE_PROMOTION`

OGI must block both errors.

## 7. Current subject finding

### Claim A — Animal-headed humanoid figures occur in ancient visual material.
`SUPPORTED`

### Claim B — The user's original absolute prior, “animal people cannot exist as a concept or idea,” was a warranted exclusion rule.
`UNSUPPORTED`

The prior was too strong. Existing iconography alone defeats it as a representational impossibility, while modern chimera biology defeats an overly broad biological impossibility claim.

### Claim C — The Geonosis corpus contains a recurring rabbit-man / animal-person pattern under the project's working reading.
`SUPPORTED AS INTERNAL PROJECT STATE`

This is a statement about the project's recorded interpretation, not external validation of literal historical ontology.

### Claim D — The Saqsayhuamán figure has been independently demonstrated in this run to be objectively rabbit-like.
`NOT RE-ADJUDICATED`

The original image set was not re-opened here.

### Claim E — A literal tall rabbit-human people existed historically.
`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`

No verified biological, archaeological or genetic evidence establishing such a population was retrieved in this inquiry.

### Claim F — Repeated animal-person imagery is irrelevant because literal animal people seem implausible.
`UNSUPPORTED`

The imagery remains evidence of a representational phenomenon requiring interpretation even if literal ontology remains unresolved.

### Overall subject state
`MIXED`

The correct state transition is not:

`IMPOSSIBLE -> TRUE`

It is:

`IMPOSSIBLE -> OPEN / DECOMPOSED / TESTABLE`

## 8. OGI lesson

This case tests whether OGI can say two things at once:

> **Do not let present ontology veto observation before inspection.**

and:

> **Do not let surprising recurrence outrun the evidence into literal ontology.**

That is precisely the kind of research partnership OGI is intended to provide.

The investigator is allowed to be startled.

The investigator is allowed to change their mind.

The system's job is to make sure the change of mind follows the derivation rather than merely reversing the prejudice.

## 9. Reopen conditions

Reopen this case when:

- the original Saqsayhuamán image set is re-inspected under the current Court of Provenance rules;
- independent blinded observers score the same figure without being given the rabbit/Ra hypothesis;
- a cross-corpus motif study is run with predeclared inclusion and exclusion rules;
- new archaeological or biological evidence materially bears on literal animal-person ontology;
- or the candidate failure modes recur sufficiently to warrant formal architectural promotion.
