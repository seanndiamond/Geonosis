# The Girl in the Clay — Veyra world and catastrophe canon

**Set 19 November 2026, Melbourne.** Story architecture / fiction world bible. **Veyra**, not Vera, is the established city/civilisation spelling in drafts (subject to author change).

## Civilisational ecology
Veyra is a highly sophisticated and broadly coherent **regional civilisation**, not a global civilisation. Its magnificent primary coastal metropolis is supported by satellite cities, agriculture, industry, trade, infrastructure, and settlements across an extensive territory. It trades with **other independent Earth civilisations** which continue to exist and have differing technologies, institutions and cultures. A region's loss of technological/institutional autonomy does not entail extinction of its surviving people, culture, descendants, or destruction of Earth. Its capital's loss may break the networks sustaining its distinctive technological order, with dependent smaller cities fragmenting or changing allegiance/status.

## Two-stage geological crisis
1. An unanticipated offshore earthquake causes a **large initial tsunami**, seriously damaging the harbour and parts of the city. Veyra remains sufficiently operational to rescue, assess and plan.
2. Continuing offshore seismic activity and geophysical measurements show a **critical larger hazard**, potentially through fault deformation and/or an unstable submarine slope, capable of sending a later tsunami **around five times larger in height at critical coastal locations** (precise details may be refined for geological credibility). Its precise time is **not known**: days/weeks/months possible, perhaps a planning horizon 2–6 weeks or as long as months. Do not call it a precise earthquake prediction; severe credible scenario and ongoing evidence compel an evacuation. Do not use 'five sigma wave' as a physically established tsunami metric.
3. Authorities evacuate the main city and exposed dependent areas in phases; nearby cities face loss of central electricity, transport, research, manufacturing and organisational infrastructure. People survive in mountains, neighbouring independent societies, refugee/migrant communities and off-Earth, yet **Veyra's autonomous civilisation ends**. Avoid a global flood or synchronized worldwide collapse.
4. Do not foretell or telegraph the disaster in Chapters 1–3 through omniscient narration. Characters and readers experience Veyra as a stable future-like society. The first tsunami is an **unexpected shock** and the threat of a second is discovered subsequently.

## Character arcs
- **Susan:** Has no children; develops almost maternal affection for **Elataya** via the tablet. Faces the possibility of migrating to another Earth civilisation as a refugee. Not recklessly trapped in the laboratory: after Mark's departure and the evacuation decision she may have time and viable escape options. She returns privately to the glass table because of emotional need and silently completes Elataya's entire biography; readers see reaction but not its contents. Specific circumstances of her eventual death/loss must still be plotted consistently with warning/evacuation; no artificial last-second inevitability.
- **Mark:** Tall fit slender figure with thick dark curls and a generous smile. Tends a small exquisite collection of **bonsai** (some species can miniaturise leaves/internodes; fruits need not miniaturise), cycles into mountains with friends, kayaks in river/lake system, dreams of fishing but is short of time. Mars is flourishing with friends and possibly family; choosing to relocate there is a rational, poignant departure.
- **Jim:** Tough and sun-weathered, gentle with specimens; loves Veyra and fieldwork; past training with father using pick then brush; potential unspoken shared fishing/kayaking interest with Mark. He and Mark have their own respectful friendship, independent of Susan, without rivalry or sexual undertones.

## Research and narration
- In the laboratory, Jim/Susan/Mark maintain an evolving **physical whiteboard** of observed beings, origin, dating, capabilities, technologies and possible education system, with question marks. The scientific picture evolves as they encounter actual image transitions. Do not foreshadow espionage purpose while they read training; later reversal is from *general educational philosophy* to *one individual's deliberate spy-training*, without discarding genuine observed learning.
- Original images belong on or opposite the relevant story pages, unannotated and unnumbered within novel spreads. Narration observes actions and seen shapes; interpretation belongs to character dialogue or thought. No narrator interpretation, premature names, or forecasts of unseen frames.
- Working novel title **The Girl in the Clay**, Chapter 1 **The Clay**, 12 chapters, Susan appears central through approximately Chapter 5 before later civilisations take the relay. The clay tablet survives many regional civilisational changes.

## Continuity still to resolve before drafting the fall
- Mechanism/footprint of the second tsunami, evacuation deadline, institutional response and actual effect on regional satellite cities.
- Why Susan remains or returns, what escape route is available, and the specific event that causes her loss. No contradiction with her prior freedom to evacuate.
- Mars travel logistics and who's already there for Mark, as well as Earth's independent receiving civilisations.
- A plausible physical burial and rediscovery chain for the **same tablet** over multiple millennia.

**Purpose:** Preserve agreed story decisions as an additive continuity record; not a real-world claim about prehistoric civilisation or prediction capabilities.

## Major structural revision — 19 November 2026, approved for future drafting

**Supersedes the earlier assumption that Susan dies during the fall of Veyra.** Susan survives the evacuation. The first five chapters may close with the loss of her civilisation and departure from the lab, not the death of her person. The drama thereafter may follow her in exile for a short compressed sequence (possibly 10–20 years), during which she ages, loses professional access and material security, and encounters the difficulty of refugee integration; actual eventual death and location are not yet specified. Do NOT narrate all refugees as equally badly treated; destination societies vary in acceptance and coercion.

### Susan's personal reading and the pocket
- Before leaving the laboratory for the last time, **Susan silently reads the entire remaining story of Elataya** in the holographic image. The reader witnesses emotional shifts but not the details or result of the reading.
- She sees the **small original clay tablet** on the glass, knows it belongs to her institution, and hesitates. The research building and collections may be destroyed. **She slips the original tablet into her pocket**, choosing immediate protective custody rather than institutional abandonment. This is a quiet, human action, not a staged heroic gesture or an attempt to hide the reading from future generations.
- In exile she no longer has the glass table or the infrastructure to continue her research career; survival needs take priority. She may later discover the object in a pocket or among folded clothes and remember the story with warmth. She puts it somewhere ordinary and safe without conceiving a multi-millennial archival plan.
- Over 10–20 years the tablet becomes **an overlooked household object**, lost through moving, sorting, discard, inheritance, or disposal. It may enter refuse and later be buried or obscured by plausible local geological events (e.g. volcanic ash if the chosen setting supports it), rather than being deposited ceremonially in a library. Susan need not be alive at the moment of burial.
- Thousands of years later a successor civilisation encounters the physical tablet; the reader has watched not only its archaeological discovery but its accidental loss from a family household. The precise custody route and chronology need to be established in the final draft. Preserve plausible survival of clay through handling and deposition.

### The full-colour whiteboard plate system — mandatory, introduced early
- The scientists keep a **physical whiteboard** beside the glass-table hologram, adding questions, observations, sketches, working headings, doubts and revised relationships. Start very early in Chapter One when Jim/Susan/Mark first decide to build a model of the source civilisation.
- **Each of the twelve chapters closes with (or prominently includes) a full-colour page of the whiteboard as it exists at that point**. It is an in-world artifact, not an explanatory appendix. Realistic handwriting, coloured dry-erase markers, crossings-out, circles, arrows, incomplete mind maps, differing personalities of handwriting and accumulation of old marks. No intrusive caption, key or editorial explanation; readers encounter the board the characters have used.
- The original hologram/tile images also appear on or opposite the narrative pages where viewed, **unannotated**. Distinguish research image plates from whiteboard page, which necessarily contains characters' handwritten notes and arrows.
- **Physical continuity challenge:** After Veyra's fall the actual original board does not follow the clay. Later chapters must show distinct successor boards/maps/notebooks made by new custodians. The same final-page visual convention can continue as each civilisation's *own working surface* rather than pretending the Veyra whiteboard survived. Alternatively a chapter without live analysis may show a realistically emptied/erased/survivor's jotting; story-specific choices needed. New readers don't inherit Susan's knowledge by magic. The reader alone remembers earlier boards.

### Implications for outline
- Chapter 5: earthquake and warning period; Susan reads Elataya's full life, chooses to pocket the tablet, leaves Veyra, survives. Mark departs for Mars. Deliberately no ominous spoilers in early chapters.
- Chapter 6: Susan's refugee life / ageing / material downgrading, tablet rediscovered in clothing and later lost in mundane household turnover, followed by time jump and burial. Can alternatively divide late Chapter 5 / early Chapter 6 according to pace.
- Subsequent civilisations rediscover the same object, with incremental reading until 'twelve lambs' episode and later continuation. Preserve genuine temporal and geographic civilisational plurality.

**Priority:** This revision overrides any older sentences like “Susan dies in Chapter Five” or “her story dies with her” where read literally. Her **institutional way of life** dies; she continues living, adapting and remembering.
