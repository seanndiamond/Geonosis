# Beyond Phonetics

## Méthodê Glyph, Picterpretation, and Geonosis as a Falsifiable Operator-First Method for Visual Knowledge Systems

**Sean Diamond**  
Independent Researcher, GlyphLens Research & Development  
Geonosis Project

**ResearchGate Preprint Version 1.0 | Prepared for publication | May 2026**

## Abstract

This paper formalises the methodological canon for Méthodê Glyph, Picterpretation, and Geonosis: an operator-first approach to analysing visual, spatial, archaeological, and terrain-based knowledge systems before reducing them to phonetic, textual, decorative, symbolic, or mythological categories.

The method begins from a disciplined delay. Marks, figures, animals, hybrid forms, postures, alignments, cavities, thresholds, terrain correspondences, and recurring motifs are first examined as possible operators within constrained visual systems. Only after such units have been inventoried, mapped, compared, stressed, and exposed to failure should interpretation proceed toward narrative, symbolic, phonetic, ritual, or cultural explanation.

Méthodê Glyph is the formal visual method for reading structure without premature narrative inflation. Picterpretation treats images as primary epistemic artefacts rather than secondary illustrations of language. Geonosis extends the method to terrain, monuments, geomorphology, settlement patterning, spatial recurrence, and the possibility that landscapes can preserve structured information across scale.

The method does not claim that all ancient visual systems are non-phonetic, nor that all landforms are artificial, intentional, encoded, or meaningful. It proposes a falsifiable procedure for asking when visual and spatial configurations behave as constrained systems rather than as random resemblance, decorative convention, or interpretive projection.

## Public method summary

The paper defines:

- observation before interpretation;
- operator inventory and relation mapping;
- evidence and confidence ladders;
- falsification and reject conditions;
- alternative-explanation testing;
- independent replication requirements;
- terrain viewing and provenance standards;
- AI-assisted audit with explicit epistemic limits;
- separation between observation, hypothesis, and speculative reconstruction.

## Governing principle

> Before asking what a form means, ask what it does.

A publishable Geonosis claim should remain reversible from conclusion to hypothesis, from hypothesis to mapped relation, and from relation to visible observation.
