# War on Ra — Four pillars of the claimant
2026-11-26 | Narrative architecture handover; preserves existing chronology.
## 1. Appropriation of magnificence
The trophy hunter admires the ram yet extinguishes its autonomy to display its beauty as his achievement. The claimant similarly desires the achievements of independent civilisations; admiration becomes possession. Conservation rhetoric may serve as social justification rather than motive.
## 2. Appropriation and recovery of agency
Elataya's relational education becomes an instrument of the king's mission. Her affection is real; the appropriation of it is the betrayal. Ren's loyalty to foundational protective law and her recovery of her life contrast with domination. On Mars the young woman approaches Anubis in love; Christ takes over her actions without extinguishing her love. Anubis recognises her first. Preserve this established scene anchor.
## 3. Capture and inversion of benevolent institutions
In the project's working Uluru reading, the dog forge was built for vigilance, companionship and coexistence. The invading consciousness captures its achievement, turning guardianship into war. This is institutional theft, not the inherent nature of the dog. The reported giant dingo and Wallaby people narrative is an unverified parallel, not an assertion of Indigenous authority or an authorised retelling.
## 4. Capture of interpretation
The archive survives but access to its meaning can be constrained by inherited interpretive authority. The child's question is central: if pictures and sounds are claimed to occupy the same marks, show how both work. The novel uses the pictographic archive as its narrative premise; no unsupported phonetic layer is required. Preserve parent readings, transformation history, and the distinction between observation, interpretation and literary elaboration. Do not present historical intentional suppression or the fictional Christ consciousness as independently established history.
## Narrative direction
Reveal the four pillars through actions, not lectures. The claimant can admire what he appropriates; Anubis can care without ownership. Intelligence is not a ladder. Do not alter the established chapter chronology or ending.
