## Non-negotiable evidence distinction
1. **Artefact:** Actual small clay tablet, accession 11.217.19 at the Metropolitan Museum of Art, catalogued as `Cuneiform tablet: receipt of lambs` (ca. 2041 BCE). That is the institution's classification, not a fictional prop.
2. **Working research:** Geonosis Méthodê Glyph interpretation from source imagery and rotation, mirroring, translation and offset overlays. It proposes a sustained account of a young female individual, provisionally designated Elataya, involving education, animal relationships, specialized abilities, covert deployment to another civilization, capture, treatment, attempted rescue and ensuing consequences. These are **research interpretations**; they are not 'fiction' because a novel was subsequently based on them. They require direct examination of plate sequences, transformation parameters, alternatives and a Court of Provenance record; they are not independently verified history by virtue of their coherence.
3. **Narrative development:** The novel *The Girl in the Clay* introduces or elaborates Veyra, Susan, Jim, Mark, Nima, reconstructed motives and dialogue, detailed training programme, Ren's dilemmas, the invented regional two-tsunami event and other scenes. These details must not be cited as archaeological proof.

The phrase `its fictional development follows a young woman whose education ...` was corrected by the author on 21 November as improperly treating the core tablet reading as invented. **Use 'our pictographic reading follows...' and 'the novel develops that reading by...'** instead.

## Transferable finding 1: agency crosses scales
Individuals can affect regional or inter-civilizational outcomes where decisions enter institutions capable of amplifying, restraining or challenging them. Model the chain **person → relationship → institutional response → network-level outcome**, allowing reciprocal feedback. Do not assume the same person is necessary/sufficient to cause a geopolitical result.

## Transferable finding 2: education is not identical to the purpose of its patron
In the novel, a sophisticated, caring institution develops Elataya into a formidable person with deep capacities for human/animal cooperation. The king's covert aim is to deploy exceptional capacity to assassinate a leader of a distinct, cohesive civilization. The genuine excellence of the education is distinct from the political misuse of the graduate.

Connection: Ra's Law of One and the Dresden Vigilant Guardian require provision **and** responsibility. The key question is: *for whose flourishing is the trained capability directed?*

## Transferable finding 3: protective law must constrain power
Ren's **fictional** dilemma is not chiefly personal sympathy or reputation management. A powerful civilization is compromised when protective principles cease to protect an unpopular or dangerous captive. His line: **'If I don't do it, it costs us everything.'** It refers to the civilization's legal and moral foundations.

The novelist has deliberately retained costs and ambiguity: extra-legal escape, harm to a guard, danger to family, exile, irreversible loss, and the difference between court protection and forcible rescue. Do not infer every act Ren takes is lawful merely because his purpose is to correct an unlawful imprisonment. His relationship with Elataya is respectful and reciprocal, not a saviour hierarchy.

## Transferable finding 4: external destruction differs from endogenous incoherence
**Veyra is fictional.** It is a regional, technologically advanced civilization, connected to many independent others. An initial unexpected earthquake/tsunami injures its metropolitan system; a subsequently identified large tsunami threat drives evacuation. People survive, some on Mars or in other Earth civilizations, but Veyra's autonomous technological/institutional system fails. This is a narrative **thought experiment**, not source-tablet proof of an actual ancient Veyra or specific tsunami.

This challenges any model claiming all collapses begin in moral decay. Compare external exposure, warning, response, resilience and the survival of autonomous functions separately.

## Transferable finding 5: archive as memory of relational consequences
Our research model proposes that image-based archives can preserve biographies and civic lessons as well as transactions. Elataya's interpreted record prompts inquiries into selection, pedagogy, loyalty, agency, judicial limits, institutional repair, war and intimate human/animal relationship.

The source object remains subject to competing interpretations. The novel's invented modern 'receipt of lambs' ending dramatizes the difference between an object's survival and the loss or narrowing of interpretive access; it is not independent disproof of conventional administrative readings.

## Transferable finding 6: understanding requires transmission
Nima asks why people do not read the ancient archive if its stories can change their lives. Susan teaches her to interpret visual relationships and then to tell those findings to people outside the laboratory.

**The archive preserves experience; the reader reconstructs relations; the storyteller returns that knowledge to living society.**

This is part of the survival mechanism: material preservation, interpretive recoverability and effective public communication are different requirements.

## Essential caution about current novel canon
The linked world bible has multiple **superseding** editorial instructions. Older notes conflict with later decisions on whether Veyra dominates six chapters or a three-chapter prologue, Susan's survival, the chronology reveal, cohort numbers and manuscript chapter locks. Preserve the latest approved narrative state. No fiction-world scene should be transferred to the scientific model as if it were an observed tablet event.
