# If the Administrative Reading Cannot Account for the Field

## Cuneiform Visible-Derivation Audit

**Sean Diamond | Geonosis Project**

**Status:** Active research programme. Updated ResearchGate paper assembly remains in progress.

## Research question

Can an accepted administrative, phonetic, or historical reading be demonstrated from the visible artefact through an explicit derivation chain?

The minimum chain under audit is:

**artefact → visible sign/feature → sign identification → assigned value → grammatical or operational relation → translated or interpreted meaning**

The presence of a citation proves that an interpretation has a source. It does not by itself prove that the interpretation can be derived from the object.

## Governing audit rules

- A caption is not a translation.
- A classification is not an audit.
- An institutional label remains a claim until its derivation can be shown.
- Pictographic and phonetic readings should receive comparable evidentiary treatment.
- Rotation, sequence, grids, recurrence, adjacency, and orientation can be tested as operations rather than dismissed or adopted by preference.
- A tablet identified as an account should be able to account for the administrative mechanics attributed to it.

For an administrative reading, relevant questions include whether the proposed interpretation visibly demonstrates recipients, quantities, units, deliveries, totals, shortages, allocations, or control logic where such features are claimed.

## Controlled programme

The current programme includes:

1. construction of BM 19377 as a full evidence record;
2. audits of early accounting and ration tablets for practical administrative mechanics;
3. visible-derivation testing of Behistun sign values and names;
4. comparison with bounded-register and recurrence behaviour in the Dresden Codex;
5. separation of visible method claims from higher-order civilisational reconstruction.

## Falsification

The Geonosis counter-reading weakens or fails where conventional sign identification and linguistic derivation can be demonstrated transparently from the artefact and account for the visible field more economically than an operator-first alternative.

Conversely, failure to expose a derivation chain does not automatically prove a pictographic reading. It reopens the object for audit.
